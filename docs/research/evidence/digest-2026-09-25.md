# Evidence digest — 2026-09-25 (week 2026-09-18..09-25)

42 endometriosis records were indexed in PubMed this window; 34 are
endometriosis-relevant and appear below (1 carried over from the previous
snapshot, 1 duplicate record, 6 dropped as noise — see the end). This is a
broad but *incremental* week: no guidance-level item, no trial result that
changes practice. The strongest items are a **population-based cohort linking
endometriosis to cardiovascular disease** (Beijing claims data, ~20,000
exposed women), **phenotype-specific obstetric risk in deep infiltrating
disease**, a **post-marketing safety analysis of Myfembree (relugolix
combination)** in FDA adverse-event reports, and the **first prospective human
data on oral cannabinoid extracts** — against an unusual cluster of
computational and -omics papers, two of which are explicit about their own
limits (a biospecimen-methodology audit and a re-analysis that refuses its own
positive enrichment signal). One PMID from the previous window recurs (the
simvastatin repurposing paper, PMID 42668641 — the 7-day pdat window
re-indexes it and it is listed as carry-over, not re-counted). Prior digest:
2026-09-18. Every entry below comes from a fetched PubMed record; PMIDs are
live.

## Diagnostics and imaging

- **Peritoneal-fluid small extracellular vesicle (sEV) proteins as candidate
  biomarkers** — case-control proteomics of peritoneal fluid (n = 63: controls,
  rASRM I–II, III–IV): four proteins differed significantly across groups —
  ALDOA (3.12× higher in controls, p = 0.0164), HSP90AA1 (2.14× controls,
  p = 0.0245), COL15A1 (1.83× controls, p = 0.0421) and S100-A10 (2.54× higher
  in endometriosis, p = 0.0421), with concentrations said to vary independently
  of cycle phase and stage. Case-control proteomics (Monash / Oxford / Warwick);
  the authors call for larger samples and functional assays.
  [PMID 42786124](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42786124/) — *why it matters:*
  peritoneal fluid is the compartment closest to the lesions and direct sampling
  needs surgery; four marginally-significant proteins in 63 samples is a
  hypothesis, not a marker panel.
- **Thoracic endometriosis — imaging series** — three women (aged 27–38) with
  histologically confirmed thoracic disease: pleural nodules, recurrent
  right-sided effusions, pneumothoraces and diaphragmatic involvement, often
  cycling with menstruation. Case series (chest radiography + contrast MRI).
  [PMID 42777115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42777115/) — *why it matters:*
  catamenial chest symptoms are the presentation most likely to be sent down a
  pulmonary rather than gynaecological path; recognising the imaging pattern is
  the whole diagnostic step here.
- **Concurrent thoracic and umbilical endometriosis, found during subfertility
  workup** — case report: a 33-year-old with three years of cyclical
  periumbilical pain and swelling plus an incidental right pleural effusion;
  umbilical nodule and pleural nodules/cysts were both histologically confirmed
  as endometriosis, and a GnRH analogue produced complete resolution, with
  recurrence of the effusion when it was stopped to attempt conception. Case
  report. [PMID 42775362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42775362/) — *why it
  matters:* extra-pelvic disease can be multifocal and silent; it also shows the
  treatment dilemma patients face — symptom control versus trying to conceive.
- **FAP expression and whether it tracks imaging visibility** — retrospective
  immunohistochemistry in deep (n = 13) and superficial (n = 9) endometriosis:
  fibroblast activation protein was substantially expressed in the
  fibro-inflammatory stroma of *both* phenotypes (median H-score 170 vs 200,
  p = 0.738) and below colorectal adenocarcinoma controls; no significant
  relationship to lesion visibility on MRI or transvaginal ultrasound (groups
  too small: 2–5 low-visibility lesions per reader). Retrospective IHC +
  imaging-correlation study (Mayo Clinic).
  [PMID 42758302](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42758302/) — *why it matters:*
  a histopathological rationale for FAP-targeted PET — importantly including
  superficial lesions conventional imaging misses — but this study does not
  answer whether FAP imaging would actually see them.
- **Accessory cavitated uterine mass (ACUM) misread as refractory
  dysmenorrhoea** — case report: a woman in her 20s with severe dysmenorrhoea
  from 1–2 years after menarche, refractory to medical therapy; ultrasound
  suggested a leiomyoma, MRI showed a non-communicating haemorrhagic
  intramyometrial lesion, and only laparoscopy revealed the accessory cavitated
  mass (with minimal pelvic endometriosis); laparoscopic excision resolved the
  pain completely at 6 months. Case report (India).
  [PMID 42785780](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42785780/) — *why it matters:*
  a structural, surgically curable differential that ultrasound mislabels —
  relevant to anyone whose dysmenorrhoea is being managed as endometriosis
  alone.
- **Provocative "estrogen hypersensitivity" test using Hsp90α** — 60 women (30
  laparoscopy-confirmed endometriosis, 30 controls): baseline plasma Hsp90α did
  not differ between groups, but 6 h after a single 2 mg oral estradiol valerate
  dose the rise was far larger in endometriosis (27.29 ± 15.62 vs 3.16 ± 4.36
  ng/mL, p < 0.001); a rise above 8.6 ng/mL gave sensitivity 0.93 / specificity
  0.97 in this sample. Single-centre case-control with a drug-challenge protocol
  (n = 60). [PMID 42762506](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42762506/) — *why it
  matters:* a novel test concept and the **third independent appearance of
  HSP90α in this week's records** (see also PMIDs 42786124 and 42782802) — but
  n = 60, single centre, unreplicated, and it requires taking estradiol: a
  research hypothesis, not a test to request.

## Treatment, drugs and safety

- **Post-marketing safety profile of Myfembree (relugolix + estradiol +
  norethindrone)** — FAERS disproportionality analysis, 2021Q2–2025Q4, 1,423
  unique reports: dominated by bleeding and menstrual-cycle events
  (heavy/intermenstrual bleeding, irregular menstruation, dysmenorrhoea);
  clinically important thromboembolic, vasomotor, hair-related and
  neuropsychiatric signals were less frequent; 10.0% of reports were serious,
  and median time to onset was **19 days** (IQR 8–55), with many major bleeding
  and vascular events inside the first weeks. Retrospective pharmacovigilance
  disproportionality study of spontaneous reports.
  [PMID 42778759](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42778759/) — *why it matters:*
  this is an FDA-approved option for endometriosis pain, and the report profile
  matches the label rather than contradicting it; spontaneous-report
  disproportionality has no denominators or incidence, so read it as signal
  timing (early weeks), not as risk magnitude.
- **Oral cannabinoid extracts (CBMP) for chronic pelvic pain — pilot** —
  prospective single-centre cohort (Charité Endometriosis Research Centre): 30
  enrolled, 27 completed 3 months of balanced THC:CBD oral extract; pain
  intensity fell markedly (p = 0.0004) with reductions in pain days, NSAID *and*
  opioid use (p < 0.001), plus improvements in fatigue, anxiety, central
  sensitization, disability and EHP-30 quality of life; side effects mild and
  mostly confined to titration. Uncontrolled prospective cohort (no placebo, no
  blinding, n = 27).
  [PMID 42771648](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42771648/) — *why it matters:*
  the most direct human data yet on a therapy patients are already
  self-managing — and a direct follow-up to last week's endocannabinoid review,
  which said clinical trials are what is missing. This is not one; expect
  expectation effects at this size.
- **N-acetylcysteine RCT in women with endometriosis undergoing ICSI** — 60
  laparoscopy-confirmed patients randomised to NAC (1.8–3 g/day, 6 weeks) or
  control: serum lead fell 0.6 µg/dL and nickel 0.8 µg/dL (medium effects), with
  total cholesterol −28.4 mg/dL and triglycerides −19.7 mg/dL (large effects),
  but HDL also fell 5.2 mg/dL, and LDL was unchanged. Randomised controlled
  trial, n = 60, surrogate endpoints only.
  [PMID 42781143](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42781143/) — *why it matters:*
  the headline effects are on lipids and heavy-metal levels, not on
  endometriosis outcomes — there is no pain, lesion or fertility endpoint here,
  and the authors themselves flag the HDL drop. Not a treatment signal.
- **Arimoclomol-loaded nanomicelles in a rat model** — 10 Wistar rats, single
  intraperitoneal dose at day 16: lesions in the loaded-nanomicelle group were
  macroscopically smaller with atrophic glands, stromal disorganisation and less
  inflammatory infiltration than empty-carrier or free-drug groups; the reported
  "100% loading" is a nominal mass-balance figure (no purification step was
  applied). Preclinical formulation study (n = 3–4 per arm).
  [PMID 42778111](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42778111/) — *why it matters:*
  a lesion-directed local-delivery concept (co-inducer of the heat-shock
  response) consistent with the local-delivery logic in OpenEndo's repurposing
  work; with 3–4 animals per arm and no quantitative lesion endpoint it is
  exploratory only.
- **Recombinant hCG versus dual trigger in poor responders with endometriosis** —
  152 women with endometriosis and poor ovarian response undergoing ICSI: dual
  triggering produced more mature (MII) oocytes, more good-quality embryos and a
  higher chance of pregnancy than r-hCG alone. Comparative cohort study, single
  centre (Tehran University of Medical Sciences); the abstract reports no effect
  sizes or confidence intervals.
  [PMID 42782241](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42782241/) — *why it matters:*
  a concrete IVF protocol decision in exactly the patient group where it changes
  counselling — but the missing numbers matter; read the paper before repeating
  the direction as a preference.
- **Depot medroxyprogesterone acetate and meningioma risk** — review of
  2018–2026 systematic reviews, propensity-matched and case-control studies:
  meningioma risk rises with prolonged DMPA use (sustained systemic progestin
  exposure), while the absolute number of cases associated with DMPA is
  described as extremely low; the authors weigh this against DMPA's role as
  treatment for endometriosis and dysmenorrhoea and call for formal
  risk-benefit assessment. Narrative review (PubMed search, no new
  meta-analysis). [PMID 42765513](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42765513/) —
  *why it matters:* relevant to long-term depot progestin use for endometriosis
  pain — and the absolute-risk framing (not the relative risk) is what belongs
  in a counselling conversation.
- **Single-cell immune profiling plus computational repurposing** — scRNA-seq of
  peripheral blood mononuclear cells (endometriosis vs unaffected controls)
  integrated with public menstrual-blood/endometrium datasets: increased B- and
  T-cell subsets and broad inflammatory dysregulation, with **HSP90AA1 and
  PLCG2** consistently upregulated; docking/MD screening prioritised
  **upadacitinib, sorafenib, alpelisib and sulindac** as hypothetical
  candidates. Computational study (scRNA-seq + pharmacophore modelling +
  docking/MD), no experimental validation.
  [PMID 42782802](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42782802/) — *why it matters:*
  reinforces the systemic-immune framing and nominates targets, but docking
  scores are predictions — and the candidates overlap drugs already on the
  broader repurposing radar. No M3 verdict moves.

## Surgery

- **Robotic excision of colorectal deep endometriosis with intraoperative
  ultrasound (video)** — single case (38-year-old with recurrent colorectal
  disease after prior rectal shaving): two lesions (sigmoid 15 × 5 mm, rectum
  24 × 9 mm) treated by linear-stapler and discoid/circular-stapler excision;
  post-excision ultrasound detected **residual disease at the rectal margin**,
  prompting immediate re-excision and confirmed complete removal — without
  segmental bowel resection. Educational surgical video / case report (Fertil
  Steril). [PMID 42767396](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42767396/) — *why it
  matters:* the intraoperative verification step is the transferable idea — it is
  the argument that "complete excision" should be checked, not assumed, when the
  goal is preserving the bowel; single case from an expert centre.

## Mechanisms and biology

- **YTHDF3–m6A–PFKFB3 axis drives angiogenesis** — human and mouse ectopic
  endometrial tissue: PFKFB3 upregulated in lesion vascular endothelium
  alongside YTHDF3; endothelial-specific *Ythdf3* deletion lowered PFKFB3 and
  suppressed lesion angiogenesis; METTL3 deposits m6A on PFKFB3 mRNA that
  YTHDF3 recognises to boost translation and glycolysis, and estrogen raises
  both METTL3 and PFKFB3 via ERα. Preclinical mechanistic study (mouse deletion
  models + human tissue). [PMID 42776305](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42776305/)
  — *why it matters:* a concrete glycolytic-angiogenesis node with a
  hormone-linked upstream switch; mechanism-level only, no therapeutic or
  biomarker claim yet.
- **A histone-cluster signal in eutopic endometrium that refuses its own
  story** — re-analysis of GSE153739 and GSE51981 (77 cases / 34 controls) with
  non-reproducible hits removed: a reproducible, proliferative-phase-concentrated
  histone-cluster signal (phase-adjusted β = 0.394, P = 0.00021) that **loses
  nominal significance once proliferation is adjusted for**, and the
  pre-specified ion-channel endpoint covaries with the same array-level
  technical axis; the authors explicitly state the biology and the technical
  component cannot be separated and that the data do **not** establish
  extracellular histone release, NETosis or a pain mechanism. Public-transcriptome
  re-analysis. [PMID 42781559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42781559/) — *why
  it matters:* an unusually honest negative, and a useful yardstick for how
  carefully endo -omics claims should be stated. There is no mechanistic claim
  to carry forward.
- **Why biospecimen expression studies in endometriosis keep failing to
  replicate** — using MAP4K4 as a test molecule: public microarray datasets
  showed small effects and inconsistent direction; MAP4K4 differed between
  pipelle and full-thickness eutopic biopsies; bulk qPCR found no group
  difference while immunohistochemistry showed higher protein in mid-secretory
  eutopic endometrium; laser-capture qPCR and in-situ hybridisation found no
  consistent compartment effect — and excised "ectopic lesions" frequently
  contained **non-endometrial tissue**. Methodological study, multi-modal tissue
  validation (Christie / Liverpool / Edinburgh).
  [PMID 42769725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42769725/) — *why it matters:*
  probably the most useful quality paper this week: it explains mechanistically
  why single-platform expression claims in this field (including several
  published ones) fail to reproduce, and it sets the bar for the tissue claims
  OpenEndo cites.
- **Cancer-associated mutations in endometriosis** — review: recurrent PIK3CA,
  CTNNB1, KRAS, PTEN and ARID1A mutations appear in endometriotic lesions with
  patterns distinct from cancer; the authors position endometriosis as a model
  for early tumour-like processes. Narrative review (Palermo).
  [PMID 42773934](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42773934/) — *why it matters:*
  frames the benign-malignant interface; no mutation currently stratifies
  patients or changes surveillance, and last week's compartment-resolved -omics
  paper argued against a progression mechanism — keep both in view.
- **Endometriosis from initiation to therapeutic pipeline** — review
  integrating clonal lesion biology with peritoneal, ovarian and deep-infiltrating
  microenvironments, cancer-associated programs (PI3K-AKT-mTOR, RAS-MAPK,
  WNT/β-catenin, hypoxia, oxidative stress, epigenetics), model systems
  (animal, organoid, 3D scaffold, microfluidic) and the non-hormonal pipeline.
  Narrative review (Cancer Lett).
  [PMID 42762962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42762962/) — *why it matters:*
  a current orientation map of the non-hormonal pipeline and the models used to
  test it — useful for the repurposing and ferroptosis tracks; contains no new
  data.
- **A "neuroimmune fitness landscape" framework** — review proposing that lesion
  persistence reflects the capacity of the local neural–immune–stromal–vascular
  microenvironment to maintain coordinated activity, and explicitly labelling
  "neuroimmune memory" an **unresolved hypothesis** requiring longitudinal
  demonstration of persistence, enhanced response to re-challenge and
  reversibility. Hypothesis-generating review (Int Immunopharmacol).
  [PMID 42763242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42763242/) — *why it matters:*
  a testable way to organise why pain and recurrence diverge from lesion burden
  — cite it as a framework, never as an established mechanism (the authors say
  so themselves).
- **Ovarian fibrosis as a shared mechanism of infertility** — review arguing
  that extra-follicular fibrosis (matrix accumulation, stromal stiffening,
  inflammation) actively constrains follicle activation, ovulation and oocyte
  quality, with endometriosis, polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome,
  obesity, reproductive aging and treatment injury converging on the same
  fibrotic state; causal support comes from hydrogel studies isolating
  stiffness, and antifibrotic finerenone has produced mature oocytes/embryos in
  early human trials in premature ovarian insufficiency. Narrative review with
  early-human off-label signal (Biol Reprod).
  [PMID 42784230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42784230/) — *why it matters:*
  reframes a component of endometriosis-related subfertility as potentially
  reversible and anti-fibrotic-treatable; the human finerenone data come from a
  different population and no study links fibrosis measurement to a fertility
  outcome — hypothesis-level.

## Epidemiology, outcomes and access

- **Endometriosis and cardiovascular disease** — historical population-based
  cohort from Beijing employee claims (2010–2017), 19,903 women with
  endometriosis versus 79,612 age/year-matched controls: adjusted hazard ratio
  **1.69 (95% CI 1.35–2.12)** for any incident CVD, 1.83 (1.32–2.55) for major
  CVD and 1.57 (1.12–2.22) for cerebrovascular disease, with no significant
  effect modification by age group; the authors report ~68% of the all-CVD
  association as statistically mediated by statins and ~78% by aspirin.
  Population-based retrospective cohort.
  [PMID 42757591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42757591/) — *why it matters:*
  one of the largest endometriosis–CVD cohorts published, and consistent in
  direction with the existing literature. The statin/aspirin "mediation"
  percentages model prescribing as an intermediate step and should be read as a
  signal about who gets screened and treated, not as a mechanism; residual
  confounding (BMI, smoking, blood pressure) is not visible from the abstract.
- **Obstetric and neonatal outcomes in deep infiltrating versus superficial
  endometriosis** — retrospective cohort, 268 singleton pregnancies at a
  tertiary centre (2015–2024; 102 deep infiltrating, 166 superficial): placenta
  previa more frequent and independently associated with deep disease (adjusted
  OR 2.41, 95% CI 1.02–5.68), as were the placenta-related composite outcome and
  NICU admission (aOR 1.89, 1.01–3.54); preterm birth was higher in the deep
  group but did not survive adjustment. Retrospective single-centre cohort
  (Istanbul). [PMID 42768257](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42768257/) — *why
  it matters:* supports phenotype-specific antenatal risk stratification — deep
  disease, not "endometriosis" generically, is the risk marker; wide confidence
  intervals and one centre.
- **Thyroid autoimmunity and reproductive outcomes (All of Us)** — large national
  biobank cohort: antibody-specific associations, with menstruation disorders
  linked to TPOAb/TgAb positivity, infertility and recurrent miscarriage the most
  robust findings, and **endometriosis showing a moderate association driven by
  TPOAb and overall antibody positivity**. Retrospective biobank cohort (NIH All
  of Us v8). [PMID 42773785](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42773785/) — *why it
  matters:* another entry in the autoimmune-comorbidity file for endometriosis;
  from an EHR-derived cohort with diagnosis-capture limits, and "moderate
  association" is not a screening indication.
- **Chronic overlapping pain conditions in refractory urologic pelvic pain** —
  152 patients with urologic chronic pelvic pain syndrome (83% female, mean age
  43) from an NIH-funded trial: mean 1.9 overlapping pain conditions; individual
  conditions were most consistently associated with wider pain distribution,
  worse physical function and fatigue, and **endometriosis showed the most
  extensive pattern of significant relationships**; migraine-containing pairs
  carried the highest illness burden. Cross-sectional analysis of trial baseline
  data. [PMID 42767279](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42767279/) — *why it
  matters:* quantifies the central-sensitisation overlap patients describe, and
  argues for brief overlapping-pain screening in refractory pelvic pain —
  cross-sectional, so direction is unresolved.
- **Carbohydrate-to-fibre ratio and endometriosis (NHANES)** — 2,400 US women
  (2003–2006 cycles): higher carbohydrate-to-fibre ratio associated with higher
  odds of physician-diagnosed endometriosis (highest quartile OR 2.17, 95% CI
  1.06–4.14; fully adjusted OR 1.02 per unit), with a non-linear turning point at
  17.3 and partial mediation by the neutrophil-percentage-to-albumin ratio
  (5.16%). Cross-sectional survey/biomarker analysis.
  [PMID 42762434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42762434/) — *why it matters:*
  a dietary association that fits the inflammation narrative, but
  cross-sectional with a single self-reported diagnosis question and a 5%
  mediation share — not a dietary recommendation.
- **Mendelian randomisation across 108 traits** — two-sample MR (UK Biobank
  exposures, FinnGen endometriosis): 12 traits showed suggestive causal
  associations, including telomere length (OR 1.29, 95% CI 1.13–1.47),
  triglycerides (1.13) and HDL cholesterol (0.89), with concordant sensitivity
  analyses for most. Genetic-instrument study.
  [PMID 42760702](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42760702/) — *why it matters:*
  moves lipids and telomere length into candidate causal territory for
  prevention research; MR rests on instrument and pleiotropy assumptions, and
  surgical-diagnosis capture in registry cases biases who counts — hypothesis-
  generating.
- **Early life trauma and endometriosis** — clinic-based case-control, 68 women
  with an existing endometriosis diagnosis recruited from a psychiatric women's
  health clinic: 86.8% reported some early life trauma (most commonly between
  ages 6–10), with emotional abuse 76.5% versus 37.3% in the general Australian
  female population, neglect 35.3% versus 10.8%. Case-control, self-report.
  [PMID 42760359](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42760359/) — *why it matters:*
  a large prevalence gap supporting trauma-aware endometriosis care — but the
  sample is a single psychiatric clinic and recall/attribution cannot separate
  cause from consequence.
- **Mediterranean diet adherence and symptoms** — cross-sectional study of 254
  women with endometriosis recruited through Spanish patient associations: each
  point of MEDAS score was associated with lower odds of pelvic pain (OR 0.85,
  95% CI 0.73–1.00, p = 0.044) with fatigue attenuated after adjustment, and
  **no independent association with any EHP-30 domain**. Cross-sectional
  validated-questionnaire study. [PMID 42759694](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42759694/)
  — *why it matters:* patient questions about anti-inflammatory diets are
  constant, and this is the honest answer — a weak symptom signal with no
  quality-of-life association.
- **Exercise advice experiences in Australia** — cross-sectional survey
  (N = 151): exercise advice came mostly from GPs (75%); for polyendocrine
  metabolic ovarian syndrome it was framed around weight control, for
  endometriosis around symptom management; women with the conditions were
  significantly **less satisfied** with the advice (p = 0.004) and **less
  confident** about exercising afterwards (p = 0.006) than women without them.
  Cross-sectional survey (Flinders / UniSA).
  [PMID 42766783](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42766783/) — *why it matters:*
  a concrete, fixable access-and-quality gap: exercise is guideline-recommended
  but the counselling is not landing — supports referral to qualified
  professionals, which is the framing OpenEndo's access content already uses.
- **Epistemic injustice in endometriosis care, gender-diverse perspective** —
  autoethnography by a nurse (assigned female at birth, gender diverse) across
  five US institutions: diagnostic delay, repeatedly minimised pain, profound
  self-doubt and internalised invalidation, compounded by gender misrecognition
  in both care and clinical documentation, despite high health literacy and
  private insurance. Autoethnography (single author).
  [PMID 42785378](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42785378/) — *why it matters:*
  names the compounding burden for trans and non-binary patients with pelvic
  pain; cite it as lived-experience testimony, not prevalence data.
- **School-based menstrual-health and endometriosis education** —
  quasi-experimental pre/post study, 75 girls (mean age 15.8) in seven French
  high-school classes: two interactive sessions combining menstrual-health and
  endometriosis education with behaviour-change activities raised perceived
  benefits of consulting a provider (p = 0.024), action planning (p < 0.001),
  maintenance and recovery self-efficacy, and progression toward medical
  consultation (p = 0.019). Quasi-experimental, **no control group**, short
  follow-up (authors' own caveats).
  [PMID 42771854](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42771854/) — *why it matters:*
  an education data point for the "delay starts young" thesis — and an
  intervention shape that awareness work could copy; uncontrolled, so effect
  size is not interpretable.
- **Nurses' role in comprehensive endometriosis care** — clinical review (from
  the Center for Endometriosis Care, Atlanta): frames nurses as direct-care
  clinicians, treatment coordinators, educators and advocates within
  multidisciplinary care, addressing physical and psychosocial needs. Narrative
  clinical review. [PMID 42765750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42765750/) —
  *why it matters:* a care-model framing usable for OpenEndo's access and
  awareness material; no data. (A second PubMed record of the same article,
  PMID 42765751, carries no abstract or affiliation and was not counted.)
- **Comorbidity clustering in POTS with endometriosis (letter)** —
  correspondence reporting that connective-tissue, mast-cell and autoimmune
  comorbidities cluster in patients with POTS who also have endometriosis
  (Cleveland Clinic). Letter; the record has **no abstract and no data**.
  [PMID 42766242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42766242/) — *why it matters:*
  touches the mast-cell/connective-tissue thread OpenEndo tracks
  (`concepts/mrgprx2-pain.md`) and a question patients raise constantly —
  but as correspondence-level evidence, cite it for the question, never for a
  number.

## Notable clinical signals

- **Myfembree (PMID 42778759)** — the item a patient on relugolix combination
  therapy might ask about: real-world reports are dominated by bleeding
  disturbances, with thromboembolic, vasomotor and neuropsychiatric signals and
  a median onset of ~19 days. These are disproportionality signals from
  spontaneous reports, consistent with the label; they do not estimate risk.
- **Cardiovascular risk (PMID 42757591)** — the largest cohort this week and the
  one with the clearest patient-facing consequence: endometriosis was associated
  with roughly 1.7× the rate of incident cardiovascular disease in a
  population-based claim dataset. It is an association, not an indication for
  new testing, but it is consistent with the broader literature and worth
  mentioning in the same breath as blood-pressure and lipid management. Ignore
  the statin/aspirin "mediation" percentages as a mechanism.
- **Pregnancy planning in deep infiltrating disease (PMID 42768257)** — placenta
  previa and NICU admission were independently associated with deep, not
  superficial, disease. Phenotype-specific antenatal surveillance is the
  practical implication; the study is single-centre with wide confidence
  intervals.
- **Nothing this week changes an OpenEndo repurposing verdict.** The
  computational repurposing paper (PMID 42782802) adds predicted candidates only,
  and the methodology paper (PMID 42769725) plus the histone-cluster re-analysis
  (PMID 42781559) both raise the evidence bar rather than lowering it.
- **One biochemical thread to watch, unvalidated:** HSP90α/HSP90AA1 appeared in
  three independent records this week — peritoneal-fluid sEV proteomics
  (PMID 42786124), single-cell target nomination (PMID 42782802) and the
  estrogen-challenge test (PMID 42762506). Nothing here is established; the
  convergence is worth a note, not a claim.
