## Diagnostics and the diagnostic pathway

- **Enhanced TVUS over a decade: strong for endometriomas, weak for
  peritoneal disease** — retrospective observational cohort, 221 patients
  with paired preoperative enhanced transvaginal ultrasound and laparoscopy
  (2015–2024) at one tertiary hospital, mapped to #ENZIAN compartments:
  adnexal endometrioma sensitivity 81.4% / specificity 84.1%; tubo-ovarian
  complex disease 72.9% / 68.8%; deep-endometriosis sensitivity lower, with
  location-dependent specificity 83–95%; bladder involvement PPV 83.3% /
  NPV 94.8%; ureteric disease PPV 0%; peritoneal disease rarely identified.
  Retrospective single-centre cohort.
  [PMID 42808571](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42808571/) — *why it
  matters:* an unusually honest accuracy map for the technique the field is
  standardising — and it says explicitly what ultrasound still misses
  (peritoneal and ureteric disease).
- **A visual ontology to make lesion recognition trainable** — systematic
  review: 932 records screened, 35 studies of histologically confirmed
  lesions at laparoscopy. Lesions grouped into 4 classes / 11 subclasses
  (superficial black/red/white/subtle; dense or filmy adhesions; deep
  obliteration/retraction/deformation; ovarian endometrioma/chocolate fluid),
  with published PPV ranges black 47–97%, red 33–100%, white 20–81%, ovarian
  42–98%, adhesions 16–50%, subtle superficial 0–67%; deep lesions often need
  indirect signs. Systematic review.
  [PMID 42433129](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42433129/) — *why it
  matters:* it is the annotation standard any AI lesion-recognition tool needs
  — and it documents how unreliable naked-eye PPV is for several lesion
  classes.
- **A standardised transvaginal strain-elastography protocol** — methodological
  study (narrative review of methodological heterogeneity plus a prospective
  observational cohort): compression control, ROI geometry, reference tissue
  and reproducibility vary widely across studies; the proposed protocol
  produced stable, interpretable elastograms at uterosacral ligaments,
  rectovaginal septum and anterior rectosigmoid, with lesions consistently
  stiffer than adjacent tissue. Protocol/feasibility study — diagnostic
  accuracy was **not** assessed.
  [PMID 42806661](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42806661/) — *why it
  matters:* stiffness is a promising deep-endometriosis adjunct, and
  measurement variability is what has held it back.
- **Symptom-reporting tools in primary care (realist synthesis)** — 102
  studies, 22,266 participants aged 13–70: independent use of symptom
  reporting tools could improve symptom awareness, health literacy,
  self-management and quality of life, and in appointments support
  communication and personalised treatment — **but without guidance it could
  also cause distress and disempowerment**, and evidence on timelier
  diagnosis was limited. Realist synthesis (PROSPERO CRD42024518266).
  [PMID 42805645](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42805645/) — *why it
  matters:* the both-directions finding is the useful one — symptom tracking
  is not automatically good for patients.
- **Shortening the diagnostic pathway: what the literature has actually
  shown** — narrative review with structured evidence mapping: 61 sources
  (1996–2026), 23 empirical reports published 2015–2026. Controlled evidence
  that any intervention reduced diagnostic delay was limited; almost no study
  measured the interval its intervention targeted, and none considered
  accuracy, patient outcomes, service demand, cost or equity alongside
  timeliness. Proposes a six-domain framework (structured symptom assessment,
  differential diagnosis, appropriate imaging, clinically justified tests,
  safety net, post-referral follow-up) with untested validity. Narrative,
  non-systematic review (no bias assessment).
  [PMID 42819538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42819538/) — *why it
  matters:* the diagnostic-delay literature is mostly feasibility and
  accuracy studies, not proof that anything shortens the wait.
- **MISE-Q: a patient-reported "most impactful symptom" questionnaire** —
  cognitive-interview study (Three-Step Test-Interview method), 34 interviews
  across five iterative rounds with surgically diagnosed participants.
  Participants broadly valued the person-centred framing, though some found
  choosing a single most-impactful symptom hard because symptoms overlap; a
  three-month recall period was supported; instrument revised (rewording, two
  symptoms and one item removed). Instrument development (content validity).
  [PMID 42813251](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42813251/) — *why it
  matters:* the endometriosis core outcome set calls for exactly this
  measure; it is still a draft, not a validated scale.

## Treatment and drugs

- **Elagolix is non-inferior to dienogest — first direct head-to-head** —
  phase III, multicentre, double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled
  non-inferiority RCT: 19 centres in India, 230 randomised (115/arm), 24
  weeks, oral elagolix 150 mg once daily vs dienogest 2 mg once daily.
  Primary outcome (change in NRS pain, baseline→Day 85) treatment difference
  0.04 (95% CI −0.3 to 0.37; p=0.9747), upper CI below the 1.5 margin;
  Day 169 comparable for overall pain, dysmenorrhoea and non-menstrual pelvic
  pain; rescue medication and PGIC comparable; adverse events 14.8%
  (elagolix) vs 19.1% (dienogest), no serious TEAEs or discontinuations; no
  significant bone-mineral-density change over 24 weeks. Phase III RCT
  (CTRI/2023/01/049292).
  [PMID 42116589](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42116589/) — *why it
  matters:* the clinically interesting item of the week: the two oral options
  patients are actually offered perform comparably on pain at 24 weeks. It
  bounds a choice that is usually made on cost, tolerance and familiarity —
  not a new drug, and 24 weeks is short for bone-density claims.
- **Simvastatin/primaquine get a human-cell assembloid follow-up (preprint)**
  — preprint (bioRxiv): human endometrial assembloids built from immortalised
  epithelial (12Z) and stromal (iEc) lines plus primary patient tissue, in
  96-well agarose molds, exposed 24 h to ibuprofen, fenoprofen, primaquine and
  simvastatin, then mRNA-seq. GSEA showed primaquine reversing the chemical
  carcinogenesis/ROS pathway; simvastatin reversing cytokine–cytokine receptor
  interaction in both epithelial and stromal lines (and the cytoskeleton-in-
  muscle-cells pathway in stroma); fenoprofen reversing calcium signalling in
  stroma. Simvastatin produced the largest expression changes, mirroring the
  same group's rat-model findings; the authors flag the small sample size.
  Preprint, preclinical.
  [PMID 42818379](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42818379/) — *why it
  matters:* it is the direct follow-on to last digest's headline repurposing
  paper ([PMID 42668641](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42668641/)) — the
  mechanism now reproduces in human-derived cells, but there is still no human
  trial and no clinical-data extension. The M3 watchlist entry is unchanged
  in status, strengthened in mechanism.
- **N-acetylcysteine RCT in endometriosis — surrogate endpoints only** —
  randomised controlled trial, 60 laparoscopy-confirmed women undergoing
  ICSI, NAC 1.8–3 g/day vs control for 6 weeks. Endpoints were serum heavy
  metals and lipids, not pain or fertility: lead −0.6 µg/dL (Cohen's d 0.62,
  p=0.03) and nickel −0.8 µg/dL (d 0.59, p=0.04); cadmium and chromium
  unchanged; total cholesterol −28.4 mg/dL (d 2.01, p<0.001), triglycerides
  −19.7 mg/dL (d 1.45, p<0.001), HDL **fell** 5.2 mg/dL (d 0.89, p=0.001),
  LDL unchanged. RCT (n=60).
  [PMID 42781143](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42781143/) — *why it
  matters:* a cheap, widely available supplement moved lab values, but nothing
  here is a patient-relevant endpoint and the HDL decrease is unexplained —
  not a treatment signal.
- **Monitoring nociception did not reduce opioid use** — prospective,
  randomised, single-blinded RCT within a standardised multimodal analgesia
  protocol: 111 analysed (NOL®-guided n=54 vs clinical-assessment control
  n=57). Total perioperative opioid consumption did not differ (adjusted mean
  difference 14 µg fentanyl / 52 µg remifentanil; p=0.8); surgery duration was
  an independent predictor of opioid use and PACU stay; treatment group had no
  significant effect. RCT (negative).
  [PMID 42329510](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42329510/) — *why it
  matters:* another honest negative — adding objective nociception monitoring
  on top of good multimodal analgesia did not change practice-relevant
  outcomes.
- **Postmenopausal endometriosis: an updated clinical approach** — review:
  2–5% of affected individuals are postmenopausal; MRI is preferred for lesion
  detection and malignancy risk; surgical excision remains the diagnostic and
  treatment gold standard; medical options are limited, with aromatase
  inhibitors the most promising; HRT is recommended after surgical or
  premature menopause before age 45, with combined estrogen–progestogen or
  tibolone preferred given possible recurrence/malignancy risk; optimal
  regimens and duration remain unclear. Narrative review.
  [PMID 42647142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42647142/) — *why it
  matters:* this cohort keeps growing with earlier and more extensive surgery,
  and it is exactly where guidance is thinnest.
- **Crocin in-situ hydrogel for lesion-site delivery (preclinical)** — an
  artificial-neural-network/ML-optimised poloxamer 407 (17.9% w/v) plus
  hyaluronic acid (0.5% w/v) in-situ gel gelling at 34 °C in 61 s, releasing
  crocin ~100% over 72 h versus ~1 h for the solution; cytocompatible to
  100 µg/mL; in vivo lesion scores fell from ~3 to ~0–1 versus control with
  reduced TNF-α and IL-6, attributed to prolonged local retention.
  Preclinical in-vitro + animal study.
  [PMID 42805008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42805008/) — *why it
  matters:* the interesting part is the delivery logic (retain drug at the
  lesion), not crocin itself; no human data and no comparison against an
  established therapy.
- **Placental factors and extracellular vesicles inhibit endometriotic cells
  in vitro** — 12Z cells treated with first-trimester placental
  explant-conditioned medium and with isolated placental extracellular
  vesicles showed significantly reduced proliferation and viability at 24–48 h
  and reduced migration and invasion; the effect was reproduced by miRNA
  mimics and four C19MC miRNAs rose in treated cells. In-vitro study.
  [PMID 42815186](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42815186/) — *why it
  matters:* a genuinely novel candidate signal with a named mediator class,
  but cell-line only — mechanism watch, not a therapy.

## Surgery and clinical management

- **Robotic vs laparoscopic endometriosis surgery: faster, not safer** —
  retrospective observational study, 520 patients (260 per arm, random sample)
  at a single high-volume tertiary centre (2018–2022); approach chosen by
  surgeon preference. Robotic-assisted surgery was associated with
  significantly shorter operative and anaesthetic times and shorter hospital
  stays, with no significant difference in complications, conversion to
  laparotomy, ICU need, transfusion or early readmission. The authors state
  case-severity differences were not formally quantified and call the
  association hypothesis-generating. Retrospective comparative study.
  [PMID 42803892](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42803892/) — *why it
  matters:* contrary to much of the earlier literature, robotic surgery came
  out faster here — but in an unadjusted single-centre comparison, so it is
  not a basis for changing surgical policy.
- **vNOTES is feasible with concomitant endometriosis — in selected
  patients** — prospective single-centre cohort of 105 consecutive patients
  undergoing transvaginal natural-orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery for
  benign conditions with histologically confirmed endometriosis (32.4% with a
  history of endometriosis): mean operative time 57.3 min, blood loss 61.9 mL,
  intraoperative complications 3.8%, postoperative 22.9% (mostly cystitis
  6.7%, surgical-site infection 5.7%), Clavien-Dindo grade III 2.9%, conversion
  3.8%, mean stay 1.9 days; 57.1% rASRM stage I and 30.5% stage II. Exclusion
  criteria: negative sliding sign, known/suspected posterior DIE, major prior
  pelvic surgery or radiotherapy. Prospective single-arm cohort.
  [PMID 42815244](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42815244/) — *why it
  matters:* the selection criteria are the message — feasibility is shown for
  patients *without* severe posterior-compartment disease, so it does not
  speak to the cases that are hardest to operate.
- **US endometriosis surgery is moving outpatient (2016–2022)** — serial
  cross-sectional analysis of nationally representative inpatient and
  ambulatory surgical databases: 70,535 weighted inpatient versus 561,894
  weighted ambulatory encounters. Inpatient volume fell 49% (14,080→7,110;
  average annual percent change −11.5%, 95% CI −14.4 to −8.7) while
  ambulatory rose 17% (73,270→85,896; +2.8%, 0.2–5.4). Inpatient cases were
  older, more comorbid, more often Medicaid-insured (20% vs 16%) and from the
  lowest income ZIP quartile (26% vs 21%), with more ovarian, bowel and
  multiorgan involvement and more complications; hysterectomy with
  salpingo-oophorectomy shifted substantially from inpatient to ambulatory
  care. National cross-sectional database study.
  [PMID 42134736](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42134736/) — *why it
  matters:* the site-of-care shift is real — and the payer/income gradient in
  who still gets inpatient surgery is an access signal worth tracking.
- **Bowel endometriosis: a Lancet review** — bowel involvement affects
  roughly 8–12% of women with endometriosis, is most often rectosigmoid and
  usually coexists with other pelvic lesions; pathogenesis is multifactorial
  (hormonal, inflammatory, immune, genetic, anatomical); presentation can
  mimic IBS or IBD and **diagnostic delays frequently exceed 7–10 years**;
  TVUS and MRI are the main non-invasive diagnostic and preoperative tools;
  combined oral contraceptives or progestogens give symptom control but are
  not curative; surgery is reserved for obstruction or severe/refractory
  symptoms; fertility outcomes remain uncertain; rare malignant
  transformation supports multidisciplinary follow-up. Narrative review.
  [PMID 42398514](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42398514/) — *why it
  matters:* the reference framing for the presentation that is most often
  mislabelled as a digestive disease.
- *(related)* **Surgery and diagnostic-pathway items in diagnostics:**
  the diagnostic-delay review is above; no other surgical item cleared the bar
  this week (see noise list for the case reports and technique letters
  dropped).

## Mechanisms and biology

- **Platelets instruct lesion fibrosis and immune privilege** — review
  integrating human, experimental and spatial-omic evidence into one
  mechanistic paradigm: cyclical bleeding seeds an immunothrombotic niche in
  which activated platelets and fibrin scaffolds organise immune and stromal
  responders; platelet-derived P-selectin, TGF-β and platelet microparticles
  act upstream of both immune dysregulation and fibrogenic stromal
  reprogramming. Quantitative histology and second-harmonic-generation
  imaging show platelet hotspots co-localising with NK suppression, CD163+
  macrophage polarisation and aligned collagen bundles; microparticle cargo
  links to myofibroblast and reparative-macrophage transcriptional programmes,
  with platelet TGF-β and microparticle cargo downregulating NK effector
  receptors (e.g. NKG2D) and biasing adaptive responses toward tolerance.
  Narrative review (authors' own spatial-omic data).
  [PMID 42470854](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42470854/) — *why it
  matters:* a second week of thrombo-inflammation framing (cf. PMID 42259236
  last window) — the "lesion as a vascular wound that never resolves" model is
  converging across groups.
- **Endometriosis as a chronic endothelial disorder** — review proposing
  microvascular thromboinflammation as a systems-level driver: endothelial
  activation, platelet hyperreactivity, innate effectors including NETosis and
  impaired fibrinolysis form feed-forward circuits generating organ-selective
  vulnerability. Proposes pairing imaging endophenotypes (molecular PET,
  quantitative perfusion MRI, flow imaging) with fluid-phase readouts (cfDNA
  topology, NET fragments, platelet transcriptomes, endothelial microparticles)
  and multi-omic modules to define reproducible endotypes; prioritises anti-NET
  and precision antiplatelet strategies. Narrative review — the systemic
  cardiovascular-risk link is a proposed framework, not a measured outcome.
  [PMID 42378824](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42378824/) — *why it
  matters:* it makes the systemic-vascular claim explicit and testable, which
  is progress — but treat any cardiovascular-risk implication as hypothesis
  until outcomes are measured.
- **CCL21 drives lesion progression through NF-κB** — patient ectopic vs
  eutopic endometrium plus a mouse endometriosis model: three hub inflammatory
  genes identified (CCL21, CFD, ACKR1); CCL21 was significantly increased in
  patient ectopic tissue and mouse lesions; knockdown in 12Z cells inhibited
  proliferation, migration and invasion, promoted apoptosis, and restrained
  NF-κB activation with lower downstream IL-6, IL-1β and TNF-α. Bench + animal
  study. [PMID 42372587](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42372587/) — *why it
  matters:* another hub-gene + pathway candidate in the inflammatory map; the
  therapeutic claim rests on one cell line and one model.
- **UCA1 lncRNA stabilises E2F1 mRNA and boosts glycolysis** — UCA1 was
  upregulated in ectopic lesions; it recruits EIF4A3 to stabilise E2F1 mRNA,
  E2F1 transactivates PKM2, and the axis drives glycolytic reprogramming;
  UCA1 knockdown suppressed glucose uptake and lactate production, reversed by
  E2F1 overexpression; RNA immunoprecipitation, pull-down, FISH and ChIP used;
  subcutaneous xenograft validation. Bench + animal study.
  [PMID 42235624](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42235624/) — *why it
  matters:* one half of this week's E2F1 pair — metabolic reprogramming is
  becoming a recurring lesion-biology thread.
- **Loss of E2F1 accumulates lipid droplets via MGLL** — patient-derived
  ectopic stromal cells showed broad lipid remodelling (phosphatidylcholines,
  lysophosphatidylcholines, sphingomyelins, acylcarnitines) with increased
  triacylglycerol, total cholesterol and lipid droplets, plus reduced E2F1.
  Integrated epigenomic and transcriptomic analysis with mechanistic
  validation showed E2F1 transcriptionally activates MGLL (monoacylglycerol
  lipase, a lipolytic enzyme); E2F1 downregulation increased lipid-droplet
  accumulation and restoring MGLL partially reversed it. Bench study.
  [PMID 42803692](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42803692/) — *why it
  matters:* the other half of the E2F1 pair, and a lipid-metabolism entry
  alongside the ferroptosis direction OpenEndo tracks — same caveat as always:
  expression-level, not a therapeutic verdict.
- **Paired, immortalised patient stromal cell lines (resource)** — paired
  eutopic (NESC) and ectopic (EESC) endometrial stromal cells were isolated
  from women with ovarian endometriosis: EESCs proliferated and
  migrated/invaded more and had an attenuated decidual response, with an
  inflammatory transcriptome (cytokine–receptor interaction, MAPK) and reduced
  ERα and progesterone receptor with relative ERβ predominance —
  progesterone-resistance-like. SV40-large-T-immortalised lines retained
  stromal identity and several disease features but acquired
  immortalisation-associated cell-cycle/DNA-replication transcriptional
  remodelling. Methods/bench resource.
  [PMID 42320595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42320595/) — *why it
  matters:* a reusable paired-cell platform for the field — with the honest
  caveat that the immortalised lines drift, which the authors document.
- **Central sensitisation on resting-state fMRI** — retrospective study, 30
  women (10 with deep infiltrating endometriosis and chronic pelvic pain, 10
  with ovarian endometriomas without pain, 10 healthy controls). Women with
  painful DIE showed significantly increased amygdala connectivity with the
  right frontal pole, left paracingulate gyrus, right frontal operculum and
  anterior cingulate versus controls, plus altered posterior
  cingulate–precuneus connectivity (FWE-corrected). Retrospective imaging
  study, n=30.
  [PMID 42033133](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42033133/) — *why it
  matters:* a measurable central-sensitisation signature that supports taking
  pain that persists "beyond the lesions" seriously — but the groups were
  hand-picked by pain status and the sample is tiny.
- **Sex-specific mechanisms of chronic pain (Science review)** — synthesises
  advances across visceral and somatic pain — sex hormones, immune–neural
  crosstalk, the gut microbiome and endogenous analgesic circuits — to explain
  why females experience heightened pain, with endometriosis named among the
  female-biased pain disorders; argues for mechanism-based precision
  therapeutics rather than describing the sex bias. Narrative review.
  [PMID 42821694](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42821694/) — *why it
  matters:* high-profile framing that treats female pain predominance as a
  mechanistic question to be answered, not a baseline fact to be accepted.

## Epidemiology, access and patient experience

- **Endometriosis is common in POTS (17.3%)** — retrospective case-control
  at an autonomic centre (2018–2024): of 1,322 female patients with POTS, 229
  (17.3%) had an endometriosis diagnosis, versus 10% in the general female
  population and 10.2% in the centre's tilt-negative cohort (both p<0.001);
  where both diagnoses existed, endometriosis came first in 68%. Retrospective
  case-control (pathology or specialist documentation).
  [PMID 42641290](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42641290/) — *why it
  matters:* a patient-visible comorbidity signal — and it runs mostly in the
  direction of endometriosis being diagnosed before POTS, which is worth
  knowing for anyone presenting with orthostatic symptoms.
- **Endometriosis and the severity of pelvic inflammatory disease** —
  single-centre retrospective cohort (2018–2023), 159 hospitalised PID
  patients, 48 (30.2%) with endometriosis on TVUS: more adnexal findings
  (95.8% vs 73.8%, mostly suspected infected endometrioma); vaginal and
  endocervical cultures negative in 72.9% of the endometriosis group versus
  42.3% STD-positivity in the non-endometriosis group (p<0.01); higher
  treatment failure (35.4% vs 17.1%). Retrospective cohort.
  [PMID 42473192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42473192/) — *why it
  matters:* in patients with endometriosis, "PID" may behave differently and
  less often be sexually transmitted — relevant to how these patients are
  treated empirically.
- **Dyspareunia and heavy bleeding flag endometriosis in IBD** —
  prospective multidisciplinary study, 82 consecutive fertile patients with
  IBD and at least one endometriosis-compatible symptom; TVUS detected
  endometriosis in 51 (62.2%), of whom 44 (86.3%) had deep infiltrating
  disease. Dyspareunia (OR 4.7, 95% CI 1.6–13.9) and heavy menstrual bleeding
  (OR 4.3, 1.4–12.9) were independent predictors on multivariable analysis.
  Prospective observational single-centre study.
  [PMID 42637594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42637594/) — *why it
  matters:* two cheap questions can trigger pelvic imaging in the IBD clinic,
  where symptoms are otherwise attributed to bowel disease.
- **Coexisting adenomyosis adds bleeding and bowel symptoms — not pain** —
  comparative cross-sectional study at two tertiary hospitals, 119 women
  (60 pure endometriosis, 59 endometriosis plus adenomyosis), all
  histologically confirmed: the adenomyosis group was older (42.3 vs 37.7
  years), reported longer menstrual bleeding (10.5 vs 6.7 days) and more
  cyclical bowel changes (55% vs 28%); the groups did **not** differ in pain
  scores, infertility, menstrual-loss categories or SF-36 quality of life.
  Older age and longer bleeding independently increased the odds of
  adenomyosis. Cross-sectional comparative study.
  [PMID 41992935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41992935/) — *why it
  matters:* useful counterweight to the assumption that co-existing
  adenomyosis means worse pain — management should follow symptom burden, not
  the imaging label.
- **Endometriosis lowers live-birth odds in single blastocyst transfer** —
  retrospective analysis of 631 single vitrified–warmed embryo transfers
  (day-5 n=541, day-6 n=90): unadjusted live-birth rates were 30.3% (day 5)
  vs 20.0% (day 6, p=0.045), but day-6 vitrification was **not** a significant
  predictor after adjustment (OR 0.63, 95% CI 0.35–1.14); endometriosis
  independently reduced live birth (OR 0.46, 0.24–0.90, p=0.02), as did
  infertility causes other than secondary/male-factor/unexplained.
  Retrospective cohort.
  [PMID 42485684](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42485684/) — *why it
  matters:* a fertility-counselling data point — and reassurance for patients
  whose only suitable embryo is vitrified on day 6.
- **Ibuprofen around pregnancy — Danish cohort** — cross-sectional study of
  24,018 pregnancies (Copenhagen Pregnancy Cohort 2013–2019): frequent
  pre-pregnancy ibuprofen use was more common with any chronic disease
  (aOR 2.40, 95% CI 1.96–2.94) and in early pregnancy (aOR 2.08, 1.31–3.32);
  **endometriosis carried one of the strongest associations with frequent
  pre-pregnancy use (aOR 4.85, 95% CI 2.14–10.99)**, alongside rheumatological
  and neurological disease. Overall use fell sharply from before to early
  pregnancy (19.8%→0.61% with chronic disease; 16.9%→0.27% without).
  Cross-sectional, self-reported.
  [PMID 42775663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42775663/) — *why it
  matters:* documents the pre-conception analgesic load in a Danish cohort —
  directly relevant to the Danish clinical context OpenEndo covers, and to
  pre-pregnancy counselling.
- **Endometriosis in transgender and gender-diverse people** —
  mixed-methods study (August 2024–March 2025), 29 participants assigned
  female at birth; mean symptom onset 15.5 years; 62.1% identified as
  nonbinary; the highest EHP-30 dimension was "control and powerlessness"
  (65.2). Interview themes: impact of pain on well-being and identity; barriers
  to trusting health-care providers; desire for collaborative care, and for
  gender-identity and racial representation in support groups. Some
  participants said the EHP-30 missed the intersection of their gender
  identity and symptoms. Mixed-methods, n=29.
  [PMID 42814108](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42814108/) — *why it
  matters:* a population the standard diagnostic-delay narrative tends to
  leave out, with a concrete critique of the most-used quality-of-life
  instrument.
- **Psychological flexibility and pain catastrophizing** — 516 women with
  endometriosis (recruited via the Italian patients' association APE):
  pain catastrophizing explained an additional 14–18% of outcome variance
  beyond demographics and pain intensity, and psychological flexibility added
  unique variance from 3% (disability) to 24–28% (anxiety, depression,
  well-being); no interaction between the two. Cross-sectional self-report.
  [PMID 42250412](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42250412/) — *why it
  matters:* two complementary, non-surgical intervention targets for the
  psychological burden — cross-sectional, so direction of effect is unproven.
- **A shared cognitive model of depression/anxiety across MS and
  endometriosis** — population-based cohorts (MS baseline n=229, follow-up
  n=134; endometriosis baseline n=399, follow-up n=130): distress tolerance
  and rumination consistently mediated the pathway from neuroticism to
  affective symptoms, distress tolerance being the most consistent mediator;
  some disease-specific differences appeared (anxiety sensitivity in the
  endometriosis depressive pathway); substantial convergence across diseases
  despite structural non-invariance. Prospective cohort, self-report.
  [PMID 42308685](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42308685/) — *why it
  matters:* supports mechanism-shared rather than disease-specific
  psychological care — and gives a named, modifiable target (distress
  tolerance).
- **Digital patient decision aids for endometriosis: low bar, AI entrants** —
  scoping review (inception to July 2025; databases, Google Scholar, grey
  literature): 10 digital decision aids met the minimum criteria — five
  expert-developed, five generated from a single prompt on conversational AI
  platforms mirroring patient/clinician queries. Most covered pharmacological
  and surgical options; quality and reporting were appraised against IPDAS and
  TIDieR, and the AI-generated aids showed notable gaps. Scoping review.
  [PMID 42788763](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42788763/) — *why it
  matters:* patients are already using AI-generated decision support; this
  documents that it mostly has not been built or reported to decision-aid
  standards.
- **China national practice survey 2024** — nationwide cross-sectional online
  questionnaire (convenience sample): 633 valid responses, 92.7%
  obstetrician-gynaecologists, over 90% attending grade or above, from 30
  provinces; 83.4% had performed or participated in endometriosis surgery;
  several indicators improved versus the 2018 survey. Cross-sectional survey
  (English abstract).
  [PMID 42802116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42802116/) — *why it
  matters:* a guideline-implementation snapshot from outside Europe/North
  America, where most of the practice-pattern data comes from.
- **AI for women's reproductive health in low-resource settings** —
  PRISMA-ScR scoping review, 116 studies (2021–2025) across six categories
  (clinical prediction, imaging, biomarker/multi-omics, reproductive and
  endocrine applications, decision support, methodology): ensemble methods
  such as random forest and gradient boosting were consistently strong, while
  convolutional neural networks performed well single-centre but degraded on
  external validation. Scoping review.
  [PMID 42789829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42789829/) — *why it
  matters:* the external-validation caveat is the finding that matters for the
  AI-diagnostics push — and it frames it for settings that would benefit most.
- **Routine dysmenorrhoea screening in adolescents** — journal item
  recommending routine screening of adolescent dysmenorrhoea to shorten time
  to diagnosis and treatment. **Title-level record only** — the PubMed entry
  carries no abstract, so no findings are reported here.
  [PMID 42707076](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42707076/) — *why it
  matters:* noted for the diagnostic-delay/awareness work; cite as a
  recommendation item, not as evidence.

## Notable clinical signals

- **Elagolix vs dienogest (PMID 42116589)** is the week's most
  practice-adjacent result: the two oral treatments patients are actually
  offered performed comparably on pain over 24 weeks, with no bone-density
  separation. It does not change what is available — it bounds the choice, and
  the 24-week horizon is too short to settle bone safety for elagolix.
- **Simvastatin (PMIDs 42818379 + 42668641)** now has human-derived-cell
  support to go with the rat-model and prescribing-data signals. Still
  **preclinical plus observational** — no trial, no dosing evidence, and the
  assembloid study is a small-sample preprint. It stays the leading M3
  repurposing watchlist item; it is not a treatment recommendation.
- **The comorbidity screening signals — POTS (17.3%, PMID 42641290), PID
  severity (PMID 42473192) and IBD predictors (PMID 42637594)** — are all
  single-centre/observational, but each gives a cheap, actionable question for
  clinicians and a real one for patients to raise. Worth a knowledge-page note
  before the next research packet.
- **Two honest negatives/surrogates:** nociception-guided opioid titration
  changed nothing (PMID 42329510), and the NAC trial (PMID 42781143) moved
  heavy-metal and lipid values, not pain — with an unexplained HDL fall. Do
  not read either as a treatment signal.
- **Diagnostics produced standardisation, not a new test:** the AI lesion
  ontology (PMID 42433129) and the elastography protocol (PMID 42806661) are
  infrastructure for reproducibility, and the diagnostic-pathway review
  (PMID 42819538) says plainly that nothing yet has controlled evidence of
  shortening delay.
- Nothing this week changes an OpenEndo repurposing verdict. No ferroptosis
  item appeared; the lipid-metabolism and glycolytic items (PMIDs 42803692,
  42235624) are adjacent to, but do not touch, the sulfasalazine/xCT
  selectivity question.
