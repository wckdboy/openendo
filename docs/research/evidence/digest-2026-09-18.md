## Diagnostics

- **NICE recommends a single saliva test to speed up diagnosis in primary
  care** — BMJ news item (2026-09-16) reporting the recommendation; the record
  carries no abstract or accuracy figures. News item, not primary evidence.
  [PMID 42749315](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42749315/) — *why it
  matters:* the diagnostic-pathway item patients will ask about first. The
  underlying test is the saliva microRNA **Endotest** (NICE guidance is
  external to this record) — read the primary accuracy papers before repeating
  any percentage.
- **#Enzian addendum — international expert survey for item generation** —
  23 experts (88.5% response): strong agreement to revise compartments B
  (uterosacral/sidewall), C (colorectal) and FB (>80%), moderate for T
  (tubo-ovarian) and FA (adenomyosis). Named proposals: suffix "d" for tubal
  dilatation in T (73.9%), suffix "P" for parametrial/pelvic-sidewall
  involvement in B (78.3%), lesion–anal-verge distance and multifocality for C,
  a graded FA1–FA3 for adenomyosis (73.9%). International expert survey (item
  generation for the Delphi rounds, not final guidance).
  [PMID 42533569](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42533569/) — *why it
  matters:* the classification OpenEndo's ultrasound-pathway content leans on
  is being extended — compartment B parametrial involvement and adenomyosis
  extent are the items most likely to change how lesions get reported.
- **Transperineal ultrasound for pelvic-floor dysfunction** — systematic
  review: 395 records screened, 17 included; consistent findings of reduced
  levator hiatal area and decreased excursion of the levator-plate and
  anorectal angles (raised pelvic-floor tone, reduced contractile capacity),
  with substantial reliability for this measurement. Systematic review,
  heterogeneous populations (chronic pelvic pain, endometriosis, vulvodynia,
  gynaecological cancers). [PMID 42735087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42735087/)
  — *why it matters:* a non-invasive way to measure the muscular component of
  pelvic pain — relevant to the physiotherapy/neuromodulation questions that
  patients bring, and the anatomical rationale behind items in this same week's
  biofeedback study.
- **BDNF as a biomarker in female infertility** — systematic review +
  meta-analysis: of 1,735 records, 20 studies entered the qualitative synthesis
  and 15 the meta-analysis; 13 of the 19 studies in women were in endometriosis.
  BDNF was dysregulated in infertility conditions, but heterogeneity was
  substantial and some analyses showed publication bias. Systematic
  review/meta-analysis. [PMID 42743156](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42743156/)
  — *why it matters:* another entry for the non-invasive-marker watchlist;
  heterogeneity and publication bias mean it is not a usable clinical marker.
- **Preoperative predictors of dense adhesions in ovarian endometriosis** —
  retrospective, 314 pathology-confirmed patients (128 none/mild vs 186
  moderate-to-severe adhesions by mASRM): neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, CA125,
  CA19-9 and fibrinogen were all higher in the dense-adhesion group; on
  multivariable analysis only age, bilateral cyst location and elevated
  fibrinogen were independent risk factors. Retrospective single-centre cohort.
  [PMID 42736801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42736801/) — *why it
  matters:* cheap routine labs (fibrinogen) as a surgical-difficulty signal;
  retrospective and single-centre, so hypothesis-level for planning.
- **Pelvic Dysfunction Composite Score (PDCS) — pilot** — three domains
  (anorectal symptoms, pelvic-floor symptoms, anorectal manometry) combined to
  0–100; 29 patients with clinically relevant pelvic dysfunction, median 45.2.
  Correlated strongly with PFDI-20, moderately with the Wexner domain and
  manometry, and significantly with pain burden (EHP-30) and sexual function
  (FSFI). Pilot, retrospective analysis of an existing cohort, no control.
  [PMID 42726219](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42726219/) — *why it
  matters:* a composite outcome measure for the bowel/pelvic-floor symptom
  cluster that current scales handle poorly; explicitly exploratory until
  validated prospectively.

## Treatment and drugs

- **Cochrane update: progesterone receptor modulators for endometriosis** —
  updated Cochrane review (CD009881.pub3, 2026-09-16; nine RCTs in total, 922
  women; **no new study eligible in this update**). All certainty of evidence
  low to very low. Mifepristone vs placebo (n=360, 3 months) may improve
  dysmenorrhoea (OR 0.07, 95% CI 0.03–0.15) but may increase hot flushes
  (OR 28.79, 95% CI 3.93–210.73); dyspareunia uncertain. Gestrinone may be
  less effective than leuprorelin for dysmenorrhoea but better for dyspareunia
  with fewer hot flushes; gestrinone vs danazol uncertain; asoprisnil had no
  extractable data. Systematic review of RCTs (co-author from Rigshospitalet,
  Copenhagen).
  [PMID 42746927](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42746927/) — *why it
  matters:* this is the reference synthesis for the PRM class — and it says
  the class still rests on one mifepristone trial plus gestrinone comparisons.
  Nothing here changes practice; it does bound what a clinician can claim.
- **Computational repurposing finds simvastatin and primaquine** —
  transcriptomics-based repositioning pipeline (UCSF/Stanford/Emory): in a rat
  model of endometriosis, both drugs attenuated pain-associated behaviours and
  reversed endometriosis-related gene-expression changes; in parallel, a
  retrospective multi-centre electronic-medical-record cohort across six
  University of California institutions found simvastatin prescription
  associated with a lower observed relative risk of an endometriosis diagnosis.
  Preclinical (animal) validation + retrospective observational cohort.
  [PMID 42668641](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42668641/) — *why it
  matters:* the most consequential paper for OpenEndo's M3 repurposing track —
  a widely prescribed, cheap oral drug with a consistent direction across a rat
  model and human prescribing data. The human signal is observational
  (indication and prescribing confounders unaddressed) and no trial exists; it
  does not move any repurposing verdict to clinical use.
- **Endocannabinoid system in endometriosis-associated pain** — review
  integrating preclinical and human evidence: cannabinoids act on three
  mechanisms in models (nociceptive signalling, inflammation via mast cells and
  peritoneal macrophages, lesion proliferation), and the authors note
  increasing self-management use among patients with endometriosis. Narrative
  review; no clinical trial data.
  [PMID 42745018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42745018/) — *why it
  matters:* the mechanism case for a question patients raise constantly; the
  review itself concludes clinical trials are what is missing.
- **Linalool in a rat endometriosis model** — 24 rats, four groups, 28 days
  of treatment: dose-dependent reduction in lesion size and oxidative stress,
  with lower NF-κB/MAPK inflammatory signalling, increased apoptosis, and
  suppressed VEGF and ICAM-1; the 100 mg/kg group showed near-normal histology.
  Preclinical animal study. [PMID 42709035](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42709035/)
  — *why it matters:* another multi-target natural-compound signal in an
  induced-lesion model — mechanistic only, no human relevance yet.
- **Menopausal hormone therapy after endometriosis-related hysterectomy** —
  two-page clinical reflection (Climacteric); the record carries no abstract.
  Title-level item only. [PMID 42747301](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42747301/)
  — *why it matters:* a real counselling gap for the growing post-hysterectomy
  cohort (residual-lesion reactivation vs symptom control), but this item adds
  no data — noted for completeness.
- **Home EMG biofeedback + electrostimulation for pelvic-floor pain** —
  prospective single-arm study: 201 participants, 107 completers. Pelvic-floor
  pain fell from 6.0 (IQR 3.75–7.25) to 1.5 (0–5.0) and dyspareunia from 7.0
  (5.0–8.25) to 5.0 (3.0–7.25) at three months (both p<0.001); dysmenorrhoea
  and dysuria also improved; cyclic/acyclic abdominal pain and dyschezia did
  not change. Using the device more than three times weekly was associated with
  larger dyspareunia reduction. Uncontrolled single-arm, 47% dropout,
  self-reported outcomes. [PMID 42734947](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42734947/)
  — *why it matters:* a home, drug-free option for the pelvic-floor component
  of pain with real-world (if uncontrolled) numbers; demands a sham-controlled
  trial before it can be recommended.
- **Endometriosis as a thrombo-inflammatory source / hydrogel
  reprogramming** — review proposing that lesions export procoagulant and
  inflammatory mediators (tissue-factor-bearing extracellular vesicles,
  activated platelets) systemically, and that injectable in-situ hydrogels
  could ablate and reprogram the lesion niche locally. Narrative review;
  the stroke-axis link is a proposed framework, not a measured clinical
  association. [PMID 42259236](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42259236/)
  — *why it matters:* a lesion-focused intervention concept relevant to the
  local-delivery logic in the repurposing track — treat the systemic claim as
  hypothesis.

## Surgery

- **Bowel surgery outcomes at 24 months (NOSE vs conventional extraction)** —
  prospective cohort, 73 women with colorectal endometriosis operated
  2019–2020: LARS scores worsened at 6 months (12.6→15.7) then fell below
  baseline by 18 months (7.4) and held at 24 (7.3); GIQLI improved and stayed
  improved; EHP-30 pain fell from 12.0 preoperatively to 2.4 at 24 months.
  Residual pain explained far more of the GIQLI variance than LARS-defined
  bowel dysfunction (adjusted R² 0.275 vs 0.182 at 24 months). Prospective
  single-cohort observational. [PMID 42742521](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42742521/)
  — *why it matters:* the most useful patient-facing message of the week for
  the surgical decision — early post-operative bowel dysfunction largely
  recovers, and pain control, more than bowel function, drives long-term
  quality of life.
- **Who actually needs segmental resection for rectal endometriosis** —
  multicentre prospective study (Leuven + Semmelweis Budapest), 196 patients,
  104 segmental resection vs 92 conservative surgery. Of five predefined
  predictors only rectal nodule length on ultrasound (OR 1.047/mm) and MRI
  circumference as % of outer contour (OR 4.543 per log2) predicted segmental
  resection; model AUC 0.756. Prospective multicentre observational study.
  [PMID 42732853](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42732853/) — *why it
  matters:* two routinely obtained measurements give a usable preoperative
  anchor for the radical-vs-conservative conversation; the authors propose
  reporting them systematically.
- *(related, not counted)* A letter to the editor in the same area (NOSE vs
  conventional segmental resection) is correspondence only and was dropped —
  see noise list.

## Mechanisms

- **NRF2 signalling as a hormone-independent axis** — review proposing a
  testable framework of "redox-adapted" and "redox-exhausted" lesion states:
  recurrent bleeding and iron accumulation create an oxidative microenvironment
  that links ferroptosis dysregulation, inflammation and defective immune
  clearance; NRF2 sits at the centre with context-dependent effects. The
  authors explicitly separate direct disease evidence from mechanistic
  extrapolation and set out translational requirements (human-lesion
  validation, predictive biomarkers, reproductive-safety assessment,
  lesion-restricted delivery). Narrative review.
  [PMID 42735734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42735734/) — *why it
  matters:* the most structured framing yet for the ferroptosis direction
  OpenEndo tracks (`ferroptosis-direction.md`) — and it names the two problems
  that keep that direction preclinical: selectivity and reproductive safety.
- **Ferroptosis-related gene panel for endometriosis** — integrative
  bioinformatics (FerrDb, DEG/WGCNA, LASSO + random forest, immune
  infiltration): six hub genes retained — BRD7, OSBPL9, AGPS, NRAS, PEX12,
  NCOA4 — plus three predicted regulatory miRNAs (miR-125a-5p, miR-218-5p,
  miR-124-3p). Computational study on public datasets.
  [PMID 42728524](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42728524/) — *why it
  matters:* a fourth ferroptosis item in three weeks, but at the gene-panel
  level — it does not touch the sulfasalazine/xCT selectivity question or any
  compound verdict.
- **Compartment-specific inflammation–metabolism remodelling** — single-cell
  (14 donors, 24 libraries, 21,600 cells) + bulk RNA-seq (24 biopsies):
  inflammatory and metabolic programmes differed by tissue and compartment;
  the myeloid inflammation-glycolysis correlation (rho 0.52) **did not
  reproduce** in bulk biopsies (rho 0.21); 31 vs 4,480 vs 4,632 differentially
  expressed genes in eutopic endometrium, peritoneal lesions and ovarian
  lesions respectively; a TCGA-UCEC cancer comparison found null survival
  effects and uncorrelated programme effects, which the authors read as **not
  supporting an endometriosis-to-cancer progression mechanism**, although
  CD24-positive cells were enriched in endometriotic tissue (4.73% vs 1.92%).
  Human tissue transcriptomics + cross-context cancer comparison.
  [PMID 42731417](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42731417/) — *why it
  matters:* argues for compartment-specific claims in the -omics work and
  pushes back explicitly on the malignant-progression narrative that the
  EAOC literature leans on.
- **SLC12A8 in the transition to endometriosis-associated ovarian cancer** —
  GEO transcriptomics + WGCNA + survival/LASSO/random-forest + immune
  profiling, with siRNA knockdown and overexpression validation: SLC12A8 was
  elevated across endometriosis and EAOC tissues, associated with poorer EAOC
  prognosis, and modulated proliferation, invasion and migration in vitro.
  Exploratory bioinformatics + in-vitro study.
  [PMID 42742760](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42742760/) — *why it
  matters:* a named candidate mediator on the endometriosis→EAOC axis; the
  authors call it exploratory and the clinical discrimination is only
  "moderate".
- **Circulating microRNAs after endometriosis induction in baboons** — plasma
  miRNA sequencing at 3, 6, 9 and 15 months after laparoscopic inoculation:
  2,731 miRNAs detected, 76 differentially expressed at least once; miR-210-3p
  rose and miR-448, miR-1260a/b and miR-1298-5p fell at all time points; early
  and late profiles separated by hierarchical clustering. Animal-model
  longitudinal study. [PMID 42417734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42417734/)
  — *why it matters:* candidate temporal biomarkers and a staged picture of
  molecular divergence — but this is an induced-lesion primate model, so
  timing and causality do not map onto human disease.
- **Aetiology of pelvic neuropathies in women** — narrative review: 306
  studies, 2,413 women, dominated by case reports and small series (no RCTs found).
  Five aetiological categories, with iatrogenic injury most frequent and nerve
  invasion/traction by disease (notably endometriosis) recurring; nerve-invasive
  endometriosis characteristically produces cyclical (catamenial) symptoms, and
  neuropathy is frequently recognised late. Narrative review, low certainty
  overall. [PMID 42738799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42738799/) — *why
  it matters:* a term-level explanation for cyclical neuropathic pelvic pain
  that sits outside the usual "it's just the lesions" framing; case-report-level
  evidence only.

## Epidemiology, access and patient experience

- **Living with adenomyosis and involuntary childlessness** — qualitative
  interview study: prolonged pathways to diagnosis with misdiagnosis and
  perceived medical invalidation, fragmented care, insufficient information and
  limited psychosocial support; being taken seriously and receiving a diagnosis
  were central to coping. Concomitant or suspected endometriosis was documented
  in a substantial proportion of participants, which the authors flag as
  limiting attribution of the burden to adenomyosis alone. Qualitative study,
  hypothesis-generating. [PMID 42736559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42736559/)
  — *why it matters:* the diagnostic-delay and invalidation pattern OpenEndo's
  access content describes now has an adenomyosis-specific account, with the
  honest caveat that endo is entangled in the same patients.
- **Endometriosis as gendered illness: body-mapping phenomenology** — six body
  maps analysed interpretively (KU Leuven / University of Edinburgh), yielding
  "conforming" bodies that internalise stigma and "resisting" bodies that
  challenge it. Qualitative (interpretive phenomenological analysis), n=6.
  [PMID 42725867](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42725867/) — *why it
  matters:* patient-voice material for the awareness/advocacy framing; no
  clinical claim.
- **Infertility cause, assisted reproduction and hypertensive disorders of
  pregnancy** — cohort study of pregnancies (conceptions 2017 onward) using
  multivariable Poisson regression: endometriosis was associated with a higher
  occurrence of hypertensive disorders **only in ART-treated pregnancies**,
  while ovulatory disorders and tubal disease carried risk independent of
  treatment. Population-based retrospective cohort.
  [PMID 42731373](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42731373/) — *why it
  matters:* a pregnancy-counselling data point (antenatal surveillance in
  endometriosis patients conceiving by ART) with a treatment-dependent, not
  disease-intrinsic, signal.
- **The new WHO infertility guideline** — FIGO commentary on the WHO guideline:
  frames infertility as a public-health priority within universal health
  coverage, and lists the remaining gaps — fertility preservation, third-party
  reproduction, male-factor infertility **and endometriosis**. Commentary.
  [PMID 42730566](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42730566/) — *why it
  matters:* endometriosis is named as an under-covered area in the global
  fertility framework — useful external framing for the access agenda.

## Notable clinical signals

- **Simvastatin (PMID 42668641)** is the one item that could eventually matter
  to patients: an off-patent, cheap oral drug with concordant rat-model and
  human prescribing data. It is **not** a treatment recommendation — the human
  finding is a retrospective EMR association and there is no trial — but it is
  the strongest repurposing candidate to appear on this cadence, and it belongs
  on the M3 watchlist.
- **The Cochrane PRM update (PMID 42746927)** is guidance-relevant in the
  negative direction: the reference synthesis for the whole progesterone-
  receptor-modulator class still rests on one mifepristone vs placebo trial
  plus gestrinone comparisons, all low or very low certainty. It bounds what
  can be claimed for PRMs; it does not change practice.
- **NICE and the saliva test (PMID 42749315)** will generate patient questions
  about a non-surgical diagnosis route. No accuracy figures are in the record —
  cite the primary evidence, not the news item.
- **Two errata** (PMIDs 42747717, 42734695) touch records used or watched
  recently — the only items this week requiring a correction sweep.
- Nothing this week changes an OpenEndo repurposing verdict; the ferroptosis
  paper (PMID 42728524) and the NRF2 review (PMID 42735734) reinforce the
  existing direction, and the compartment-resolved -omics study (PMID 42731417)
  argues against the endometriosis→cancer progression mechanism.
