## Diagnostics and imaging

- **Second-line MRI after expert ultrasound for complex deep disease** —
  prospective observational cohort. Women with suspected deep endometriosis
  scheduled for surgery had transvaginal ultrasound on the IDEA protocol.
  MRI was used selectively for suspected advanced disease, inconclusive
  ultrasound, or advanced symptoms despite a negative ultrasound. Surgery
  was the primary reference; histology was secondary when available. Of 221
  women with suspected deep disease, 189 had surgery and 112 had both
  ultrasound and MRI; 55 had previous abdominal surgery. Treating either
  positive test as positive, combined imaging raised sensitivity for
  rectosigmoid disease by 10.3 percentage points (6 true positives and 3
  false positives) and for uterosacral-ligament involvement by 29.2
  percentage points (14 true positives and 12 false positives). Specificity
  fell for endometriomas (true negatives 18 to 14, −19.0 percentage
  points), pouch-of-Douglas obliteration (14 to 8, −13.3), and uterosacral
  ligaments (35 to 23, −20.3). Accuracy for bladder and ureters fell from
  82.2% to 75.7%. Most lesions missed by ultrasound were in the mid or
  upper rectum. Rectosigmoid sensitivity of the combination was higher in
  women with prior surgery; the authors call that subgroup exploratory.
  [PMID 42922369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42922369/) — *why it matters:* a selected add-on, not a reason to order MRI for everyone. More
  detection came with more false positives. It does not change the limit
  already recorded on 2026-10-23 (PMID 42885805): a normal scan does not
  exclude superficial disease.
- **Laparoscopic phenotypes and missed preoperative suspicion in Kinshasa** —
  retrospective review of one surgeon's gynecologic laparoscopies at three
  sites, January 2019–June 2026. Of 1,743 operations, 216 (12.4%; 95% CI
  10.9–14.0) had endometriosis. Superficial peritoneal disease was recorded
  in 139/216 (64.4%), ovarian disease in 137/216 (63.4%), and deep disease
  in 68/216 (31.5%); 104/216 (48.1%) had more than one phenotype. Among 135
  women with superficial disease and classifiable preoperative records, 83
  (61.5%; 95% CI 53.1–69.3) had no documented suspicion of any
  endometriosis; in isolated superficial disease that was 46/56 (82.1%).
  In a clinical model, infertility was associated with higher non-suspicion
  (adjusted prevalence ratio 1.59; 95% CI 1.07–2.35) and deep dyspareunia
  with lower non-suspicion (0.65; 95% CI 0.45–0.94). After extra adjustment
  for coexisting operative phenotypes, the infertility estimate was 1.39
  (95% CI 0.95–2.04) and dyspareunia 0.70 (95% CI 0.49–1.00).
  [PMID 42924039](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42924039/) — *why it matters:* a surgical series, not a prevalence survey. Absence of a
  written suspicion is a documentation and referral finding. The adjusted
  ratios are exploratory.
- **Blood counts, hs-CRP, and formula-derived ROMA in endometriosis** —
  single-centre comparison, August 2019–March 2023, of 125 women with
  surgically and histologically confirmed endometriosis and 80 controls
  without clinical or ultrasound evidence of endometriosis. Preoperative
  white-cell count, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, CA-125, HE4, and
  formula-derived ROMA values were higher in the endometriosis group (all
  p<0.001). Biomarker levels did not differ across surgical procedures.
  Menopausal status was not consistently available, so the authors say the
  ROMA figures are formula-derived rather than patient-specific clinical
  classifications. The abstract reports no AUC.
  [PMID 42947085](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947085/) — *why it matters:* another internal blood-marker difference. It is not a
  diagnostic standard, and the ROMA numbers are not a malignancy
  classification. Epub 2026-07-31; indexed this week.
- **Serum miR-1271-5p with transvaginal ultrasound** — 115 people with
  endometriosis and 90 healthy volunteers. The abstract says the
  combination had high diagnostic performance and does not report an AUC,
  sensitivity, or specificity. miR-1271-5p negatively regulated IGF1R in
  cell assays (hEM15A and 12Z). Inhibiting miR-1271-5p reduced
  proliferation, migration, and invasion, and silencing IGF1R reversed
  that. Serum IGF1R mRNA was lower in the endometriosis group and inversely
  correlated with miR-1271-5p.
  [PMID 42949228](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42949228/) — *why it matters:* a methods-and-marker paper whose accuracy figures are not in
  the record. Healthy volunteers are not the differential of pelvic pain.
  Not a test to use.

## Surgery

- **Expectant management of asymptomatic diaphragmatic endometriosis** —
  cross-sectional retrospective series of 55 people in whom diaphragmatic
  endometriosis was found at laparoscopy for pelvic disease and was not
  resected. Fifty-four were asymptomatic for diaphragmatic disease; one
  had symptoms. Mean age 32.7 years. Dysmenorrhea was the most common
  symptom (81%). Coexisting endometrioma was present in 94.5% and deep
  pelvic endometriosis in 98.2%; 75% had evidence of ovarian chocolate-cyst
  leakage. Bilateral diaphragmatic disease was associated with a larger
  right endometrioma and larger rectovaginal disease. No one received
  postoperative hormonal therapy. Over a mean 34 months, none of the
  asymptomatic patients developed diaphragmatic symptoms, and the one
  symptomatic patient reported less chest pain. Second-look laparoscopy in
  four people with severe diaphragmatic disease showed improvement in three
  and progression in one. #Enzian was used to describe findings.
  [PMID 42934651](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42934651/) — *why it matters:* one series, not a protocol. The authors' statement that
  diaphragmatic disease regresses after pelvic resection is their reading
  of this follow-up. Four second looks are not a natural-history study.
  Epub 2026-07-30; indexed this week.
- **Hemoglobin drop after deep endometriosis surgery** — retrospective
  single-centre linkage in Opole, Poland, of first eligible surgeries in
  2020–2025. Decline was the last preoperative hemoglobin within 7 days
  minus the nadir through postoperative day 3. The ≥3 g/dL cut was
  operational, not a validated minimum important difference. Among 424
  patients, preoperative hemoglobin was under 12 g/dL in 41/419 (9.8%) and
  under 13 g/dL in 152/419 (36.3%). Of 416 with both measurements, 156
  (37.5%) met the cut; that count depends on which postoperative values
  were recorded. Each extra hour of surgery had odds ratio 1.61 (95% CI
  1.35–1.91) for the cut, and 1.50 (95% CI 1.28–1.76) in a model without
  preoperative hemoglobin. Continuous decline was 0.20 g/dL greater per
  hour (95% CI 0.13–0.26). Among 379 with resection-count data, the
  unadjusted frequencies were 14.5%, 38.3%, and 54.9% after no, one, and
  multiple full-thickness resections.
  [PMID 42941597](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42941597/) — *why it matters:* an association with duration, not a transfusion rule. The
  operative-extent gradient is exploratory. Preoperative anemia still
  matters because the same drop lands lower.
- **Neuropelveology review** — narrative review. The authors describe
  endometriosis-associated pain as overlapping nociceptive, neuropathic,
  and nociplastic phenotypes, and neuropelveology as structured nerve
  assessment, pain mapping, and nerve-sparing decisions. They say it may
  complement usual evaluation in selected people with refractory pain,
  neuropathic symptoms, a mismatch between symptoms and imaging, or
  suspected sacral-plexus or sciatic involvement. They also say current
  evidence is limited and that this is not a replacement for established
  management. No new cohort size is reported.
  [PMID 42941690](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42941690/) — *why it matters:* a framing paper. It does not show that nerve mapping changes
  outcomes. Prospective studies are what the authors say is missing.
- **Primary umbilical endometriosis mistaken for a urachal fistula** — one
  case report. A 31-year-old woman had umbilical discharge for more than a
  year, mostly during menstruation, sometimes bloody. Examination showed
  mild erythema without active discharge. CT of the lower abdomen and
  pelvis showed no obvious abnormality. The working diagnosis was urachal
  fistula with possible infection. Excision histology showed endometrial
  glands and stroma under squamous epithelium with hemosiderin; CD10, CK7,
  and PR were positive and ER was negative or weak.
  [PMID 42947878](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947878/) — *why it matters:* a single extrapelvic case. Cyclic umbilical bleeding was the
  clue. It is not a diagnostic pathway.
- **Malignant transformation of uterosacral deep endometriosis** — one case
  report. A 41-year-old woman with four years of worsening secondary
  dysmenorrhea and more than a year of dyspareunia had surgery for deep
  disease, including uterosacral and rectal resection, four years after a
  clinical diagnosis of adenomyosis. Pathology of the uterosacral ligament
  was well-differentiated endometrioid adenocarcinoma, FIGO grade 1,
  arising in pelvic endometriosis. Completion surgery found a metastatic
  deposit in a right obturator node. The report assigns FIGO 2009 stage IV
  and describes direct invasion of the rectal wall. She received six cycles
  of paclitaxel and carboplatin every three weeks. The report says there
  was no sign of recurrence more than five years after treatment.
  [PMID 42952678](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42952678/) — *why it matters:* one person's course. It does not establish how often deep
  disease becomes cancer, and it is not a surveillance schedule.

## Mechanisms and biology

- **Iron in endometriosis-associated macrophages** — cyst-fluid ionomics,
  single-cell RNA sequencing, and a mouse model using senescent red cells
  as a stand-in for retrograde menstruation. Iron was the element most
  elevated in endometriotic cyst fluid. A lesion macrophage subcluster
  co-expressed M2-associated markers and ferroptosis-related genes.
  Phagocytosis of those red cells raised HIF-1α and HO-1, intracellular
  iron, lipid peroxidation, and reactive oxygen species, with M2-like
  polarization and weaker phagocytosis. Those macrophages suppressed CD8+
  T and NK cell functions, in part through CXCL12–CXCR4. In mice,
  deferoxamine and ferrostatin-1 partly reversed the macrophage shift,
  restored phagocytosis, eased lymphocyte suppression, and reduced ectopic
  lesion growth. The authors call the study exploratory and say macrophage
  iron metabolism needs validation in larger independent cohorts. The
  abstract does not give the human sample size.
  [PMID 42920128](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42920128/) — *why it matters:* a macrophage and iron experiment, not a trial of a drug. Mouse
  lesion shrinkage after a ferroptosis inhibitor is a different compartment
  from the lesion-cell ferroptosis direction already on the public
  explainer. That direction, and the sulfasalazine watchlist, are
  unchanged. Epub 2026-07-27; indexed this week.
- **Microbiota-linked LysoPC(16:0) and M2 macrophages** — mouse
  endometriosis model combining 16S sequencing and untargeted metabolomics.
  Peritoneal lysophosphatidylcholine (LysoPC(16:0)) rose with a gut
  microbiota shift; fecal transplantation linked the two. LysoPC(16:0)
  promoted M2 polarization through PPARγ in two mouse strains. Macrophage
  depletion blocked the lesion growth driven by LysoPC(16:0). IL-10
  neutralization reduced lesion weight. Conditioned medium from treated
  THP-1 macrophages increased migration of primary stromal cells from
  people with endometriosis; GW9662 or IL-10 neutralization reversed that.
  [PMID 42920945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42920945/) — *why it matters:* a mouse metabolite axis. It is not a probiotic or a diet
  result, and it is not a treatment.
- **Exosomal PDLIM3 and M2 polarization** — GEO analysis plus cell and
  animal work. PDLIM3 was higher in ectopic endometrium. Exosomes from
  ectopic stromal cells delivered PDLIM3, which the authors link to NAA40
  stability, PRMT1, and M2 polarization, and then to stromal proliferation
  and migration. In the animal model, exosomal PDLIM3 accelerated
  endometriosis progression through that macrophage shift.
  [PMID 42921169](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42921169/) — *why it matters:* preclinical. A proposed target is not a drug. Issue date 2026
  Dec; indexed this week.
- **Sex hormones and pain, preprint** — 60 women previously diagnosed or
  having laparoscopy for diagnosis. Pain over 30 days, pain interference,
  and the EHP-30 pain subscale were collected. In 38, lesion and control
  peritoneum were biopsied. ESR1 transcript was higher in lesions; ESR2 was
  not different. Lesion ESR1 and PGR were negatively associated with pelvic
  pain intensity; after false-discovery correction the ESR1 association was
  no longer significant. No other tissue receptor or serum estradiol,
  prolactin, progesterone, or testosterone measure was associated with the
  pain outcomes. The authors' one-sentence summary is that
  endometriosis-associated pain is largely unrelated to those serum levels
  and receptor transcripts.
  [PMID 42926381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42926381/) — *why it matters:* a preprint, n=60, not a peer-reviewed trial. It does not say
  hormone treatment has no effect. It says these measured levels were poor
  indicators of pain in this sample.
- **Aging marks in blood: endometriosis versus associated ovarian cancer** —
  comparison of blood biological age, telomere length, and DNA methylation
  in age-matched people with endometriosis, endometriosis-associated
  ovarian cancer, and unaffected controls. The abstract gives no sample
  size. Endometriosis showed age acceleration of +16.92% and longer
  telomeres (13.18±0.46 kbp). The cancer group was biologically younger
  than chronological age (−14.64%) with shorter telomeres (12.45±0.72 kbp;
  p<0.001). A random-forest classifier used gene signatures. The authors
  describe this as a tool for predicting malignant transformation.
  [PMID 42928289](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42928289/) — *why it matters:* the abstract's precision-oncology sentence is ahead of the
  reported sample. No n, no external validation. Not a blood test. Epub
  2026-07-27; indexed this week.
- **Shared hub genes with ovarian clear-cell carcinoma** — GEO microarray
  reanalysis. Differentially expressed genes: 1,390 in fertile women with
  endometriosis, 4,223 in infertile women with endometriosis, and 2,762 in
  clear-cell carcinoma. Seventeen hub genes were shared by the infertile
  endometriosis set and carcinoma, and twelve by the fertile set and
  carcinoma. The authors name NCOA3, BIRC5, and EPHX2 as candidate
  biomarkers. They say batch correction was applied, the sample is small,
  and age, BMI, ovarian-reserve measures, and infertility factors were
  incompletely available, so the result is preliminary.
  [PMID 42929003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42929003/) — *why it matters:* a gene list from public arrays. Not a classifier and not
  evidence that endometriosis becomes clear-cell carcinoma in these
  people.
- **Adhesions after ablating ectopic cells in mice** — syngeneic transplant
  model with diphtheria-toxin ablation of donor progesterone-receptor
  cells. Ablation removed detectable GFP-positive ectopic lesions.
  Collagen and α-SMA in adhesion-associated tissue fell relative to
  established lesions but stayed above sham. Nesting and marble-burying did
  not differ significantly between toxin and vehicle; the authors note the
  cohort may have been too small to see a partial behavior change.
  [PMID 42929462](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42929462/) — *why it matters:* in this model, removing the ectopic cells did not erase
  adhesion fibrosis or normalize the behavior assays. Mouse data only.
  Publication date 2026-10-31; indexed this week.
- **12Z cells and estradiol** — in the passage range studied, estradiol did
  not significantly change 12Z proliferation, wound closure, or single-cell
  migration. The cells did respond in physically confined 3D migration.
  Raising ESR1 did not increase functional estradiol sensitivity.
  [PMID 42929847](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42929847/) — *why it matters:* a methods warning about a common cell line. It is not a
  statement about estrogen treatment in people.
- **Stromal miR-423-5p and EMP2** — GSE7305 (ectopic tissue from 9 people
  with endometriosis and endometrium from 7 without) plus paired ectopic
  and eutopic tissue from 10 people with endometriosis. M2 markers CD206
  and IL-10
  were higher in ectopic tissue and EMP2 was lower (all p<0.05).
  miR-423-5p was higher in ectopic-derived primary stromal cells. A
  luciferase assay showed direct binding to the EMP2 3' UTR. Overexpression
  raised CD206 and IL-10 and lowered EMP2; inhibition did the opposite.
  Wound healing increased with miR-423-5p upregulation.
  [PMID 42935196](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42935196/) — *why it matters:* a small tissue series plus cell assays. Not a circulating
  test. English-language abstract of a Chinese-language article; the
  Chinese full text was not re-read.
- **Molecular-crossroads review** — narrative review. The authors argue
  that endometriosis is several connected processes (hormonal, immune,
  vascular, epigenetic, metabolic) rather than a single estrogen disorder,
  and that management still relies on symptomatic hormonal suppression.
  They discuss the gap between animal models and clinical results, and
  between symptom control and disease modification. No new trial or
  accuracy figure is reported.
  [PMID 42939539](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42939539/) — *why it matters:* a map of targets, not a new therapy. Investigational nodes stay
  investigational.
- **Fetuin-A in a rat model** — after implants were confirmed, intraperitoneal
  Fetuin-A versus saline. The primary lesion-area endpoint stayed lower
  after adjustment for baseline area. Serum IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α were
  lower, and AMH was lower. Implant HMGB1 mRNA was markedly lower; OPN
  mRNA rose modestly. Staining for Fetuin-A, p62, caspase-3, and SOD1
  increased, and VEGF and OPN staining fell. The authors say HMGB1
  protein, RAGE/TLR4/NF-κB, and specific fibrosis markers were not
  measured, so this is not proof of HMGB1-pathway blockade or an
  anti-fibrotic effect.
  [PMID 42939956](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42939956/) — *why it matters:* a rat dosing experiment. Lower AMH in the treated animals is
  reported, not explained as a clinical safety claim. Not a candidate to
  use.
- **CRMP4 and Cav1.3 stability** — hEM15A cells and animal models. Less
  ubiquitin-proteasome degradation raised Cav1.3 stability. ZNF460
  increased CRMP4, which reduced RNF138–Cav1.3 association, decreased
  Cav1.3 ubiquitination, and was associated with less apoptosis in the
  cell line.
  [PMID 42942060](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42942060/) — *why it matters:* a cell-line mechanism. The suggested axis is not a drug target
  with human evidence in this record.
- **Green-tea polyphenols, computational** — network pharmacology and
  docking, plus a transcriptomic check that ESR1 was down in ectopic
  endometrium. Twenty metabolites, 292 overlapping targets. Docking:
  epicatechin gallate to ESR1 −7.84 kcal/mol; marmesin to PGR noted; an
  EGCG–EP300/CREBBP path proposed. CYP19A1 is called a plausible extra
  node. The authors say the polyphenols merit experimental evaluation.
  [PMID 42942199](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42942199/) — *why it matters:* docking and public transcriptomes. It is not evidence that
  green tea treats endometriosis, and it is not a suggestion to take it.
- **TG2 in endometrial mesenchymal stem cells** — cells from people with
  endometriosis had higher TG2 protein and lower enzyme activity than cells
  from controls without endometriosis. Membrane TG2 associated more with
  integrin β1 and syndecan-4. shRNA knockdown of TG2 removed the extra
  proliferation, migration, and invasion and brought MMP-2 and MMP-9
  activity back toward the control-cell baseline. The abstract does not
  give the donor count.
  [PMID 42943217](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42943217/) — *why it matters:* a cell-adhesion experiment. "Candidate biomarker" in the title
  is not a validated blood or tissue test.
- **Galectins when deep disease and adenomyosis coexist** — preliminary
  ELISA study. Thirty-three women with laparoscopically and
  histologically confirmed endometriosis and 14 without. The groups
  included deep disease, deep disease plus adenomyosis, and adenomyosis.
  In deep disease coexisting with adenomyosis, detectability of galectin-1,
  galectin-3, and galectin-9 changed, and asprosin, FSTL1, and TNF-α
  concentrations differed. The abstract does not give the concentrations.
  [PMID 42947782](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947782/) — *why it matters:* n=33, preliminary, no clinical cutoff. It does not show that
  these proteins distinguish the two diseases in practice.
- **Intrathecal EP4 antagonist in mice** — daily intrathecal E7046 in a
  mouse endometriosis hyperalgesia model, n=4 per group. Pro-inflammatory
  cytokines fell 30–40% versus saline-treated endometriosis mice. Spinal
  neuronal and glial activation fell. The authors say further mechanistic
  and translational work is still needed.
  [PMID 42948656](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42948656/) — *why it matters:* four mice per group and an intrathecal dose. Not a pain
  treatment for people.

## Epidemiology and burden

- **Digital tools across the course of care** — narrative review. The
  authors group apps, electronic symptom capture, electronic
  patient-reported outcomes, telehealth support, and digital pain
  interventions as the more mature uses, and imaging, risk, molecular,
  fertility, and recurrence models as technically active but usually
  limited to internal validation. They propose a clinician-supervised
  framework and say routine use still depends on external validation,
  calibration, prospective pathway studies, and accountable oversight. No
  new accuracy figure is reported.
  [PMID 42921513](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42921513/) — *why it matters:* a maturity map. An app or a model in this review is not a
  diagnostic device.
- **Suspected endometriosis without histologic lesions, mood, and cannabis** —
  clinic-based BEME study at one Los Angeles hospital, 454 patients.
  Compared with histologically confirmed endometriosis, clinically
  suspected disease with negative pathology had higher odds of depression
  (adjusted odds ratio 2.27; 95% CI 1.03–5.03). Compared with confirmed
  endometriosis, controls were less likely to use cannabis for pain
  (adjusted odds ratio 0.32; 95% CI 0.16–0.64). Compared with confirmed
  endometriosis, the negative-pathology group was more likely to use
  cannabis for depression (1.92; 95% CI 1.01–3.65). The authors say the
  cannabis figures lack dose, are observational, and sit next to known
  concerns about long-term use.
  [PMID 42926793](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42926793/) — *why it matters:* pain and depression can be present when biopsies do not show
  lesions. The cannabis associations are not evidence for or against using
  it.
- **Burden at ages 15–29 in China and the Western Pacific, GBD 2023** —
  modeled incidence, prevalence, and years lived with disability,
  1990–2023, for the region and 31 countries and areas. Regional prevalent
  cases fell from 1,876,232 (95% uncertainty interval 1,104,449–2,841,601)
  to 1,333,780 (794,707–1,989,545). Incident cases fell 32.3% and years
  lived with disability 28.7%. In 2023 New Zealand had the highest
  age-standardized rates and China the lowest rates with the largest
  absolute numbers. Average annual percent change was about −0.57% to
  −0.52% in China and −0.36% to −0.31% in the region; the authors say
  every 95% confidence interval excluded zero. Age-specific prevalence and
  disability rose from 15–19 to 25–29. Socio-demographic index was
  inversely correlated with age-standardized rates (p<0.001). Most of the
  drop in absolute numbers was attributed to population change.
  [PMID 42936750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42936750/) — *why it matters:* modeled estimates, not a registry count. The authors say a
  falling rate should not be read as a matching fall in clinical need.
- **Structural reproductive anomalies and later endometriosis** —
  population cohorts from the Australian Longitudinal Study on Women's
  Health (GELLES), 8,563 women born 1989–95 or 1973–78. Endometriosis was
  self-reported and checked against linked health records. Structural
  anomalies were associated with endometriosis: adjusted relative risk
  1.88 (95% CI 1.53–2.30) in the 1973–78 cohort and 1.63 (1.25–2.13) in
  the 1989–95 cohort. In the older cohort, site-specific estimates were
  uterine 1.86 (1.48–2.35), cervical 1.77 (1.20–2.62), and vaginal 3.23
  (1.39–7.55). Congenital uterine anomalies were 2.48 (1.72–3.59) and a
  retroverted uterus 1.66 (1.25–2.20). Estimates attenuated slightly after
  excluding women with corrective surgery.
  [PMID 42950453](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42950453/) — *why it matters:* an association, including for a positional variant, not a
  screening program. Self-report plus linkage is not surgical confirmation
  for every row.
- **Depressive symptoms in people interested in online therapy** —
  cross-sectional online survey. Of 329 participants interested in an
  online cognitive behavioral therapy program, 322 had treatment data:
  85 (26.4%) had never used hormonal therapy. Moderate to severe depressive
  symptoms (Beck Depression Inventory-II) were reported by 152 (46.2%).
  Of 265 who completed a structured clinical interview, 73 (27.5%) met
  criteria for a mental disorder, most often a depressive disorder (37;
  14%). A greater count of treatments used was associated with more
  pain-related impairment (B=2.06, adjusted R²=0.05) and more
  control/powerlessness (B=2.24, adjusted R²=0.05), Holm-adjusted p<0.001.
  [PMID 42954255](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42954255/) — *why it matters:* these percentages are for people who opted into a therapy
  study, not for everyone with endometriosis. The treatment-count
  associations explain about 5% of variance. They are not a reason to
  avoid treatment.
- **Interoceptive beliefs** — 247 people with an endometriosis diagnosis
  and 98 without. The endometriosis group reported noticing more internal
  signals, trusting them less, worrying about them more, and distracting
  themselves from them more. Higher pain intensity predicted higher
  awareness of internal sensations. The authors say the pattern resembles
  other painful conditions.
  [PMID 42955882](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42955882/) — *why it matters:* a questionnaire difference. It is not a new pain treatment and
  not specific to endometriosis until compared more widely. Issue date
  2026 Nov; indexed this week.

## Pharmacology and supportive care

- **IVF versus surgery for infertility** — multicenter pragmatic partially
  randomized patient-preference trial at three Italian referral centers
  (NCT04743167). Eligible women were under 40, with regular cycles,
  ultrasound-diagnosed ovarian and deep endometriosis, endometriomas
  smaller than 4 cm, no hydrosalpinx, ureteral stenosis, or sub-occlusive
  bowel symptoms, normal semen analysis, and no prior endometriosis surgery
  or IVF. IVF was up to three complete cycles. Surgery was followed by an
  attempt at natural conception. The primary outcome was conception leading
  to live birth within 12 months. The planned sample was 206. Recruitment
  (March 2021–September 2024) stopped after 130 women (55 randomized, 75
  preference) because investigators judged success markedly more likely
  with IVF and ethics committees agreed. In the randomized cohort, live
  births were 7/27 (26%) after surgery and 16/28 (57%) after IVF
  (p=0.019); relative risk 2.20 (95% CI 1.08–4.50). In the preference
  cohort, 7/25 (28%) and 32/50 (64%) (p=0.003); relative risk 2.29 (95% CI
  1.18–4.43). Combined relative risk 2.29 (95% CI 1.41–3.70).
  [PMID 42925945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42925945/) — *why it matters:* the week's comparative trial, and it was stopped early on the
  investigators' impression rather than a reported pre-specified boundary.
  The exclusions (prior surgery, prior IVF, larger endometriomas, ureter or
  bowel compromise) are the population. It is not a protocol, and the
  public pages checked for this digest do not currently tell people to
  choose surgery over IVF.
- **GnRH antagonists versus agonists, five randomized trials** — systematic
  review and meta-analysis of trials comparing a GnRH antagonist with
  leuprolide-based agonist treatment (PROSPERO CRD420261289010). Five
  trials. Compared with antagonist, agonist produced larger improvements
  in dysmenorrhea (standardized mean difference 0.69; 95% CI 0.48–0.91),
  nonmenstrual pelvic pain (0.33; 0.16–0.50), overall pelvic pain (0.25;
  0.13–0.38), and dyspareunia (0.14; 0.02–0.26), plus better scores the
  authors report for pelvic tenderness, pouch induration, peak pain,
  analgesic use, and menstrual blood loss. A subgroup suggested a larger
  analgesic difference after 12 weeks. Antagonist treatment produced less
  bone-mineral-density decline (standardized mean difference 0.64; 95% CI
  0.39–0.89), with a stronger bone-sparing contrast at 24 weeks. Most
  quality-of-life domains did not differ. The authors say differences
  among individual antagonists should be considered.
  [PMID 42953143](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42953143/) — *why it matters:* a class comparison against leuprolide, not against combined
  contraceptives or progestins, and not a switch order. It does not change
  the Ryeqo-versus-Yselty page or the second-line note on the GnRH
  antagonist knowledge page.
- **Elagolix systematic review** — search to September 2024. From 103
  records, 20 were included (phase II/III trials, extensions, reviews,
  network meta-analyses, real-world analyses, postmarketing reports, and
  cost studies). The abstract says screening followed PRISMA and quality
  appraisal used AMSTAR 2. Elagolix reduced dysmenorrhea (responder rate
  up to about 75%), nonmenstrual pelvic pain (about 55–69%), and
  dyspareunia (about 60%) versus placebo, with benefit described as
  sustained at 12 months. Higher doses gave more relief and more
  hypoestrogenic effects, mainly hot flushes and bone-mineral-density
  loss. With estradiol/norethindrone acetate add-back, bone loss stayed
  under 2% per year in the abstract's summary. Network meta-analyses in
  the included set are described as comparable or superior to other oral
  GnRH antagonists. A US cost comparison versus leuprolide favored
  elagolix, and patients favored the oral route. The authors say long-term
  safety past 48 months, head-to-head trials against first-line hormones,
  and non-US cost studies are still needed.
  [PMID 42937421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42937421/) — *why it matters:* a review of an approved antagonist, with approximate responder
  rates. It is not a new trial and it is not linzagolix. The Yselty page
  is unchanged.
- **Estetrol/drospirenone in recurrent endometriosis or adenomyosis** —
  retrospective chart review. Twenty-three women started estetrol 15 mg
  with drospirenone 3 mg; 14 with recurrent endometriosis and/or
  adenomyosis who completed at least 16 weeks were analyzed. Mean visual
  analogue pain fell from 7.7 (95% CI 6.9–8.6) to 3.3 (2.3–4.3), p<0.005.
  The abstract also reports an average responder rate of 55.8% and says
  85.7% had a reduction of at least 30%. In 11 people with labs, estradiol
  fell from 214 pg/mL (95% CI 34.7–394.1) to 42.7 (2.23–83.2), p=0.024.
  CA-125's downward trend was not significant. No serious adverse events.
  Eighty-six percent reported improved quality of life.
  [PMID 42951459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951459/) — *why it matters:* 14 people, no comparison group, and two different responder
  figures in the same abstract. It does not overturn the unpooled
  estetrol/drospirenone review in digest 2026-10-23 (PMID 42892004). Not
  a reason to switch regimens.
- **Motor-cortex rTMS for pelvic pain** — prospective single-centre cohort
  of clinically indicated repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation.
  Ten left primary-motor sessions over two weeks (10 Hz, 80% resting motor
  threshold, 1,500 pulses). Forty-two women with confirmed endometriosis
  and chronic pelvic pain enrolled; 40 started; analyses used 37 for pain
  and 35 for activity interference. Model-based mean pain fell from 5.69
  to 4.69 (change −1.00; 95% CI −1.46 to −0.55). Interference fell from
  6.03 to 4.63 (change −1.39; 95% CI −1.84 to −0.95). Both stayed lower at
  week 8. Of 34 evaluable participants, 10 had at least a 30% pain
  reduction and 3 had at least 50%. Of 400 planned sessions, 386 were
  delivered; 33/40 attended all ten and 38/40 at least eight.
  Twenty-eight participants reported 47 treatment-emergent events; one was
  severe, none serious, all resolved. There was no sham or untreated
  comparator.
  [PMID 42942272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42942272/) — *why it matters:* feasible delivery and a modest before-after change. The
  authors say the change cannot be attributed to stimulation. Not a
  treatment to adopt from this cohort.
- **IVF/ICSI counseling review** — narrative review of studies from 2021 to
  2026, plus selected earlier papers, organized by phenotype, adenomyosis,
  prior surgery, and cumulative live birth. The authors describe
  endometriosis mainly as a quantitative ovarian constraint (lower
  anti-Müllerian hormone, lower antral follicle count, fewer oocytes),
  especially with endometrioma or prior cystectomy, and say fertilization
  and embryo competence are generally preserved. Implantation may be
  modestly lower with some phenotypes, especially adenomyosis. They say
  endometriosis does not preclude IVF/ICSI and that counseling should
  emphasize cumulative outcomes and avoiding unnecessary ovarian surgery.
  [PMID 42937228](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42937228/) — *why it matters:* a synthesis for counseling, not a new cohort. "Avoid
  unnecessary ovarian surgery" is the authors' strategy sentence, not a
  rule adopted here.
- **Infertility mechanisms and management review** — narrative review of
  anatomy, ovarian reserve, oocyte competence, inflammation, medical
  suppression, fertility-sparing surgery, intrauterine insemination, and
  IVF. The authors single out, as questions with uneven evidence, surgery
  for endometrioma before assisted reproduction, prolonged GnRH-agonist
  pretreatment, fresh versus frozen transfer, and intracytoplasmic sperm
  injection without male-factor infertility. Anti-angiogenic,
  immunomodulatory, antioxidant, stem-cell, and epigenetic ideas are
  described as investigational. They say no single protocol fits everyone.
  [PMID 42941812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42941812/) — *why it matters:* a review of unsettled choices. It does not pick a winner, and
  it does not change the IVF-versus-surgery trial above into guidance.
- **Fertility preservation review** — narrative review, literature search
  updated to 9 August 2026. Oocyte vitrification is described as the most
  used technique; embryo cryopreservation depends on circumstances; ovarian
  tissue cryopreservation has a limited disease-specific role. Observational
  studies associate older age and previous ovarian surgery with fewer
  retrieved and mature oocytes. Ovarian endometriosis is more consistently
  tied to a lower response than superficial or deep disease without ovarian
  involvement. Antagonist and progestin-primed stimulation are both
  described as feasible. Live birth from warmed oocytes depends on age and
  oocyte number. Coexisting adenomyosis can still affect implantation and
  pregnancy after eggs are stored. Many people conceive without using the
  stored material. The authors say current evidence does not support
  fertility preservation for every woman with endometriosis, and that the
  incremental benefit over later treatment is unknown.
  [PMID 42942430](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42942430/) — *why it matters:* a limit, not a new offer. Selective counseling is the authors'
  conclusion. It is not an instruction to freeze oocytes.
- **Emerging therapies review** — narrative review covering oral GnRH
  antagonists, selective progesterone-receptor modulators, aromatase
  inhibitors, epigenetic and immunomodulatory ideas, antioxidants,
  anti-angiogenic drugs, microbiome approaches, neuromodulation devices,
  and surgical technique. The authors say conventional care suppresses
  symptoms or excises lesions and is not curative, and that many newer
  ideas rest on small studies and a heterogeneous disease.
  [PMID 42923893](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42923893/) — *why it matters:* a catalogue. Nothing in it is a completed phase III result
  new to this week.
- **Estrogen-only therapy after hysterectomy, two cases** — a 48-year-old
  woman, after surgery for stage IA grade 1 endometrioid endometrial
  carcinoma at 44, switched from combined menopausal hormone therapy to
  transdermal estrogen only. About three years later an enlarging
  ileocecal mass was resected and was endometriosis. A 54-year-old woman
  had hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy for endometriosis
  at 46, used transdermal estrogen only for about six years, and one year
  after stopping was found to have atypical endometrial hyperplasia in
  vaginal-cuff endometriosis. The authors say this does not establish that
  estrogen-only therapy caused the lesions. Occult disease, residual
  disease, or surgical implantation remain possible. They say a new
  abdominal mass or bleeding after hysterectomy still needs histology, and
  that hormone therapy should follow current guidelines.
  [PMID 42923654](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42923654/) — *why it matters:* two cases. They are not a ban on estrogen-only therapy and not
  a new regimen. Issue date 2026 Dec; indexed this week.
- **Australian Prescriber: diagnosis and management** — narrative clinical
  review. Symptoms listed include pelvic pain, dysmenorrhea, heavy
  bleeding, fatigue, and subfertility, with overlap between endometriosis
  and adenomyosis. The authors say general practitioners are often first
  contact, and that the current Australian guideline supports a clinical
  diagnosis and early management without waiting for surgical confirmation.
  First-line management in the review is analgesia and hormonal therapy,
  fitted to priorities, fertility plans, comorbidities, and other
  medicines. No new trial result is reported.
  [PMID 42936234](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42936234/) — *why it matters:* a restatement of an existing guideline, not a new guideline.
  It matches the imaging-first limit already in digest 2026-10-23 (PMID
  42885805). It does not change the public explainer's sentence that
  laparoscopy remains the standard for definitive diagnosis, and it does
  not change the saliva-diagnostics page.

## Notable clinical signals

- **No new guideline, Cochrane update, or regulatory decision** this week.
  Nothing here changes an OpenEndo repurposing verdict. Nothing here is
  adopted as a patient-facing treatment recommendation. Knowledge pages for
  Yselty and for saliva diagnostics are unchanged. The lesion-cell
  ferroptosis sentence on the public explainer is unchanged.
- **In a stopped-early trial, IVF led to more live births than surgery for selected women with endometriosis.**
  In the randomized cohort, live births were 16 of 28 with IVF and 7 of 27
  with surgery (PMID 42925945). The preference cohort pointed the same way.
  Enrollment stopped because the investigators thought IVF was already
  clearly ahead. The eligible group excluded prior surgery, prior IVF,
  endometriomas of 4 cm or larger, and ureter or bowel compromise. That is
  the result. It is not a protocol.
- **Second-line MRI after expert ultrasound found more deep lesions and more false positives.**
  The rectosigmoid and uterosacral counts are in PMID 42922369. The authors
  describe a selective role, not a routine one.
- **GnRH agonists versus antagonists (PMID 42953143)** is five randomized
  trials against leuprolide. Agonists improved pain scores more.
  Antagonists lost less bone density. That comparison does not reorder
  first-line hormones, and it does not separate one oral antagonist from
  another.
- **The Australian review (PMID 42936234)** repeats clinical diagnosis
  without waiting for surgery. It is not itself a guideline. **Elagolix
  (PMID 42937421)** is a review of an already approved drug, with
  approximate placebo contrasts. **Estetrol with drospirenone (PMID
  42951459)** is 14 charts. None of these is a new option to adopt.
- **Motor-cortex stimulation (PMID 42942272)** lowered diary pain by about
  one point in a before-after series of 37, with no sham. Ten of 34
  reached a 30% pain reduction. The authors refuse a causal claim. That
  refusal stands.
- **Structural anomalies (PMID 42950453)** carried adjusted relative risks
  of 1.88 and 1.63 for later endometriosis in two Australian cohorts,
  including a retroverted uterus. That is an association. It is not a
  screening instruction. **Depression scores (PMID 42954255)** come from
  people who wanted an online therapy program.
- **The macrophage iron experiment (PMID 42920128)** is the item closest to
  an existing OpenEndo research sentence. Mice treated with deferoxamine
  or ferrostatin-1 grew smaller lesions. The public explainer's direction
  is about lesion cells evading ferroptosis. This paper is about
  macrophage iron handling in a preclinical model. It does not move
  sulfasalazine off the watchlist and it does not rewrite that sentence.
- **Case reports and computational papers** stay at that level. Two
  estrogen-only cases (PMID 42923654) do not show causation. Umbilical
  disease (PMID 42947878) and one uterosacral carcinoma (PMID 42952678)
  are single histories. Green-tea docking (PMID 42942199) is not a dietary
  treatment. Diaphragmatic expectant follow-up (PMID 42934651) is one
  series of 55, with four second looks.
