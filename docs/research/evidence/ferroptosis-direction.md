# Ferroptosis direction in endometriosis — verdict

> Task: Jaeger's checkpoint "ferroptosis direction deep-dive" (unblocks the
> sulfasalazine candidate). Question: **does ferroptosis drive or protect
> endometriosis lesions — and is a ferroptosis-inducing drug (sulfasalazine)
> the right direction?**
>
> **Verdict: the mechanism is DOUBLE-EDGED, and the balance of current
> evidence tilts toward ferroptosis-related signaling being
> disease-SUPPORTING (paracrine/angiogenic/immune-remodeling) — blunt
> ferroptosis induction (sulfasalazine) is the WRONG-direction bet with
> the evidence we have. Confidence: MEDIUM.** (Hypothesis synthesis from
> model-level literature; no clinical data in endometriosis.)

## 1. The primary paper (the trigger)

**SEMA3C promotes endometriosis progression by inducing ferroptosis and
enhancing viability/migration of endometrial stromal cells** — Li Y, Zhang Y.
Gynecol Obstet Invest, 2026-09-01. PMID 42678895 · doi:10.1159/goi/aczag005.

- SEMA3C ↑ in ectopic vs control tissue (P<0.001), correlates with stage
- SEMA3C overexpression → **↑ACSL4, ↓Nrf2, ↓GPX4, ↓SLC7A11** (pro-ferroptotic
  shift) AND **↑ HESC viability + migration** (pro-disease phenotype)
- **SEMA3C knockdown OR Ferrostatin-1 (ferroptosis INHIBITOR) both
  suppressed viability/migration** (P<0.01) — i.e., blocking ferroptotic
  signaling reduces the pathogenic phenotype

Reading: in this model, the ferroptosis-related oxidative/inflammatory state
does not simply kill cells — it accompanies and supports a
disease-promoting phenotype. Fer-1 rescue argues **against** ferroptosis
induction as therapy in this axis.

## 2. The double-edged landscape (why the answer isn't clean)

| Direction | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Induction kills ectopic cells (pro-therapy) | erastin & derivatives induce multi-pathway death in ectopic stromal cells | Frontiers narrative review, 2026 |
| **Inhibition slows progression** | "inhibition of ferroptosis might not only inhibit progression but also improve iron-overload effects" | *Double-edged roles of ferroptosis in endometriosis and infertility*, Cell Death & Disease |
| **Ferroptotic cells FEED the lesion** | endometrial stromal cell ferroptosis **promotes angiogenesis via paracrine VEGFA/IL8** | Nature (endo angiogenesis paper) |
| **Ferroptosis remodels immune microenvironment → progression** | bioinformatics: ferroptosis promotes progression via immune remodeling | Frontiers (bioinformatics study) |
| Iron overload = hallmark | periodic bleeding → iron accumulation drives oxidative stress | multiple reviews |

## 3. What this means for sulfasalazine (xCT inhibitor → ferroptosis inducer)

1. **Mechanistic red flag:** inducing ferroptosis in stromal cells can trigger
   paracrine VEGFA/IL8 → angiogenesis that supports the lesion (Nature) —
   a feed-forward risk a blunt inducer does not address.
2. **SEMA3C-axis caution:** the freshest mechanistic paper shows ferroptotic
   signaling co-occurs with enhanced viability/migration; Fer-1 (inhibitor)
   was protective in that model.
3. **Erastin school is real but unproven at lesion level:** reviews argue
   induction potential, but no lesion model demonstrating net benefit
   accounting for paracrine effects was found.
4. **Dose toxicity adds weight against:** xCT-active sulfasalazine doses
   (4.5–6 g/day) carry real toxicity (2010 glioma trial termination;
   2026 phase 1 only tolerable in short 3-day pulses).

## 4. Verdict summary

- Ferroptosis in endometriosis is **context-dependent and double-edged** —
  no single direction.
- The balance of mechanistic evidence (SEMA3C/Fer-1, Nature angiogenesis,
  immune-remodeling, CDD review) tilts toward **ferroptotic signaling
  supporting lesion progression via paracrine/inflammatory routes**.
- **Sulfasalazine: downgraded WATCHLIST → WRONG-DIRECTION (confidence
  MEDIUM)** for endometriosis as a monotherapy. The xCT/SLC7A11 axis remains
  a valid research target — but the drug is the wrong tool for the
  mechanism as currently understood.
- Better bets the literature suggests: **ferroptosis-INHIBITION strategies**
  (Fer-1 class), **anti-angiogenic combination**, or pathway-targeted
  approaches (GPX4/Nrf2 restoration) — all hypothesis-stage, model-level.
