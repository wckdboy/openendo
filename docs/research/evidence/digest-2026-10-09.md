## Diagnostics and imaging

- **State-of-the-art imaging for endometriosis and adenomyosis** — narrative
  review (epub and issue date 2026-09-09; indexed this week, not in the
  2026-09-11 snapshot). Transvaginal ultrasound is described as the first-line
  modality, with an expert structured approach (sliding sign and a
  compartment-by-compartment look) for deep disease, especially rectal and
  bladder involvement. The Morphological Uterus Sonographic Assessment (MUSA)
  consensus is cited for adenomyosis. MRI is described for soft-tissue
  contrast and for parametria, ureters, nerves and bowel-muscle invasion
  beyond ultrasound. #Enzian is named as the shared reporting language.
  Narrative review; the abstract gives no new accuracy figures.
  [PMID 42838882](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42838882/) — *why it
  matters:* a current map of imaging practice OpenEndo's pathway content
  already leans on. It does not issue a new guideline and adds no performance
  numbers.
- **Diagnosing endometriosis in adolescents with ultrasound and MRI** —
  scoping review: 35 sources (9 primary studies, 26 secondary). Primary
  evidence was limited, heterogeneous and mostly from specialist settings.
  Ultrasound was the sole or initial modality in 8 of 9 primary studies; MRI
  was used in 4. Findings were most consistent for ovarian endometrioma and
  posterior deep endometriosis. Superficial disease was poorly captured, and
  normal imaging was common in symptomatic cohorts. Acceptability, consent
  and refusal were often discussed in secondary sources and rarely measured.
  No adolescent-specific primary study of AI-assisted imaging was found.
  Scoping review. [PMID 42833036](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42833036/)
  — *why it matters:* the primary evidence behind adolescent imaging is thin
  and largely adult-derived. The review's own limit is the one to keep: a
  negative scan, particularly for superficial disease, does not exclude
  endometriosis when symptoms persist. That is a bound on what a scan can
  claim, not a new pathway.
- **Machine-learning model for deep infiltrating endometriosis** — retrospective
  single-centre study (2020–2025): 250 surgically confirmed deep-infiltrating
  cases and non-deep endometriosis controls, split 7:3 for training and
  internal validation. Seven predictors (age, BMI, CA125, CA199,
  neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, albumin, ovarian endometrioma). In the
  validation set, AUCs were 0.787 (random forest), 0.740 (gradient boosting),
  0.685 (support vector machine) and 0.709 (logistic regression). Random
  forest accuracy 0.714, specificity 0.821. Internal validation only.
  [PMID 42835735](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42835735/) — *why it
  matters:* another non-invasive deep-disease classifier. The authors state
  that this performance does not yet support clinical readiness and that
  external multicentre validation is still required. Exploratory; low
  certainty for any diagnostic claim. Epub 2026-10-01, indexed after the
  2026-10-02 snapshot was built, so it was not in that digest.
- **Prediction models for endometriosis-associated ovarian malignancy** —
  systematic review and meta-analysis: 18 reports (search to 24 May 2025),
  risk of bias with PROBAST. Pooled AUC 0.907 (95% CI 0.874–0.932) on
  development or apparent-performance sets and 0.915 (95% CI 0.880–0.941) on
  internal validation. The two independent external or temporal evaluations
  were not pooled. Threshold-defined maximum tumour diameter (OR 1.85, 95% CI
  1.64–2.08) and intratumoral blood flow (OR 3.90, 95% CI 1.73–8.80) had
  positive pooled associations; dysmenorrhoea was not statistically
  significant. Calibration was inconsistently reported (no study reported a
  calibration intercept or slope). Every included study was rated overall
  high risk of bias. Systematic review/meta-analysis.
  [PMID 42835309](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42835309/) — *why it
  matters:* high discrimination on internal numbers is not a usable test.
  The authors' conclusion is that routine use is premature. This is an
  ovarian-cancer stratification literature, not an endometriosis treatment
  result, and it does not change care guidance. Epub 2026-09-23; indexed
  this week and absent from the prior snapshots.

## Surgery

- **Robot-assisted versus conventional laparoscopy** — systematic review and
  meta-analysis (PubMed and EMBASE, 2000–2026): 1,067 records screened, 16
  studies pooled with random effects. Conventional laparoscopy had a shorter
  operative time (mean difference −26.47 minutes, 95% CI −39.41 to −13.54;
  I²=89%; 14 studies; 2,450 patients). No significant difference was found
  for intraoperative bleeding, haemoglobin loss, conversion to laparotomy,
  hospital stay, intraoperative complications, fistula, or overall
  postoperative complications. Postoperative pain, voiding dysfunction and
  drainage could not be pooled (many zero-event studies); in the single study
  that reported pain, incidence was 40.5% in the robotic arm versus 76.7% in
  the laparoscopic arm. The time difference favoured laparoscopy in ovarian
  (4 studies, 533 patients), unspecified-site (8 studies, 1,605) and bowel
  (2 studies, 312) subgroups. Egger's test did not indicate publication bias
  for bleeding (p=0.103) or operative time (p=0.704).
  [PMID 42830120](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42830120/) — *why it
  matters:* the operative-time signal is directionally consistent and
  statistically heterogeneous (I²=89%), and most other perioperative outcomes
  are unseparated. It does not establish that one technique is superior, and
  it is not a guideline.
- **Intraoperative cyst rupture in early-stage ovarian cancer, by
  endometriosis association** — retrospective single-centre cohort of 239
  patients with apparent FIGO stage I epithelial ovarian cancer: 75
  endometriosis-correlated (ECOC), 46 endometriosis-incidental (EIOC), 118
  with no endometriosis (NEOC). Intraoperative cyst rupture was more common
  in ECOC (20%) than EIOC (13%) or NEOC (5%) (p=0.006); ECOC was the only
  factor independently associated with rupture. Rupture was not associated
  with worse progression-free survival or higher peritoneal relapse, and ECOC
  itself was associated with better progression-free survival and a lower
  risk of peritoneal recurrence. A minimally invasive approach was associated
  with a higher risk of progression; the authors say that association may
  reflect confounding, selection, or tumour biology. Retrospective
  single-centre oncology cohort.
  [PMID 42826790](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42826790/) — *why it
  matters:* an endometriosis-stratified finding inside early ovarian-cancer
  surgery, not an endometriosis treatment study. Low certainty, and it does
  not change endometriosis care.

## Mechanisms and biology

- **PRDX4 and oxidative stress in ovarian endometriosis** — immunohistochemistry
  showed higher PRDX4 in ectopic endometrium of ovarian endometriosis.
  Overexpression increased migration and reduced hydrogen-peroxide injury
  (higher viability, lower intracellular H2O2, less apoptosis); replacing the
  active cysteines Cys124 and Cys245 with serine removed that antioxidant
  effect. Overexpression decreased PTP1B oxidation and downstream
  phosphorylation. In vivo, PRDX4 promoted growth of ectopic lesions.
  Preclinical (cells and an in vivo lesion model).
  [PMID 42848172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42848172/) — *why it
  matters:* a redox mechanism on the ovarian-endometriosis lesion. The
  authors suggest that inhibiting PRDX4 could be a treatment strategy; that
  suggestion is preclinical only and is not a therapy. Low certainty.
- **SRC-2 in endometriotic epithelial cells** — steroid receptor coactivator-2
  was expressed predominantly in epithelial cells of ectopic lesions in
  primate and mouse tissue. In an immortalized human endometriosis epithelial
  line, siRNA silencing of SRC-2 reduced viability, clonogenicity, migration
  and invasion. RNA-seq pointed at actin-cytoskeleton programmes; S100A10 was
  SRC-2-responsive and required for those same cellular properties. Cell-line
  study with animal expression data.
  [PMID 42836692](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42836692/) — *why it
  matters:* a coregulator hypothesis for the ectopic epithelial phenotype.
  "Potential target" language in the paper is a reason to keep looking, not
  a drug or a test.
- **Fibrosis, tissue stiffness and pain** — narrative review. Pain severity
  does not necessarily track lesion size or extent. The authors connect
  chronic inflammation, myofibroblasts and extracellular-matrix accumulation
  to mechanotransduction, nerve remodelling and nociceptor sensitisation, and
  they propose a "pain-fibrosis niche" as a conceptual frame. They state that
  how this differs by lesion phenotype and anatomical site still needs
  investigation. Narrative review; no new primary data.
  [PMID 42844237](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42844237/) — *why it
  matters:* a mechanism sketch for pain that is out of proportion to visible
  disease. Conceptual only. Publication date is month-level ("2026 Oct"), so
  the day-bounded pdat query missed it; it was indexed this week.
- **Gut microbiota, immunity and host metabolism** — narrative review framing
  endometriosis as a multisystem state (microbial composition and barrier
  function, innate and adaptive immune changes, carbohydrate/lipid/amino-acid
  metabolism) rather than a pelvis-only lesion. No new primary cohort.
  Narrative review. [PMID 42840992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42840992/)
  — *why it matters:* a systems hypothesis aimed at biomarkers and
  pathway-targeted ideas. The abstract does not report a validated marker or
  a trial. Epub 2026-09-22; indexed this week and not in the prior snapshots.
- **In-silico CXCR4 phytochemicals (naringenin)** — docking, pharmacokinetic
  prediction, pharmacophore mapping, density-functional theory, two
  independent 100 ns membrane molecular-dynamics runs, and MM/GBSA. Eighteen
  phytochemicals were screened. Naringenin had the most favourable predicted
  docking score (−6.361 kcal/mol) versus apigenin (−5.927) and the
  co-crystallized ligand IT1t (−5.155), with predicted contacts at Tyr116,
  Arg188 and Tyr255. Predicted LD50 was 2000 mg/kg (toxicity class 4). The
  authors state that receptor-binding assays, functional validation and in
  vivo confirmation are absent. Computational only.
  [PMID 42842599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42842599/) — *why it
  matters:* a named natural-product hypothesis on a receptor the authors link
  to endometriosis and ovarian-cancer invasion. It is not experimental
  evidence, it is not an OpenEndo M3 candidate, and it does not move any
  repurposing verdict. Epub 2026-10-07.
- **Pathology-validated immortalized ovarian-endometriosis epithelial line** —
  two clinically suspected ovarian cystic lesions were immortalized (CDK4R24C,
  cyclin D1, TERT). Postoperative histology called one an endometrioma and
  the other a mature cystic teratoma. The endometrioma-derived line (iEn1)
  kept proliferative capacity, epithelial markers, a stable 46,XX karyotype,
  epithelial–mesenchymal marker co-expression, and formed microscopic
  glandular structures in an estrogen-supplemented xenograft. Estrogen
  receptor β and interleukin-6 were not detected in the original tissue or
  the xenograft, so that inflammatory phenotype is not a property of this
  model. The teratoma-derived line showed chromosomal instability.
  Methods/model paper.
  [PMID 42835858](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42835858/) — *why it
  matters:* a usable endometrioma epithelial model, with an explicit warning
  that immortalization without histology can misclassify the tissue. Not a
  clinical result. Epub 2026-09-23; indexed this week.

## Epidemiology, access and patient experience

- **Leaving Canada for endometriosis care** — anonymous online survey of
  Canadian citizens or permanent residents, aged 18 or older, with suspected
  or confirmed endometriosis, who had considered or obtained care abroad.
  559 responses, 348 analysed. Most were aged 25–44 (83.6%, n=291), identified
  as White (83.3%, n=290) and held a bachelor's degree or above (56.6%,
  n=197); 33.1% (n=115) reported household income of at least $150,000 CAD.
  70.7% (n=246) had sought surgical care abroad, and 80.5% (n=280) were
  dissatisfied or very dissatisfied with care in Canada. 96 respondents
  (27.6%) had received surgery abroad, most often in Romania (n=34, 35.4%)
  or the United States (n=32, 33.3%). The main reasons given were long waits
  (n=79, 82.3%) and a belief that Canadian surgeons lacked sufficient
  expertise (n=67, 69.8%). Of those treated abroad, 89.6% (n=86) were
  satisfied. Mean direct cost $24,395.55 CAD and mean indirect cost $7,211.11
  CAD. Self-selected online survey, not a population rate.
  [PMID 42843518](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42843518/) — *why it
  matters:* a documented access failure (waits, cost, cross-border surgery)
  in people who could finance it. It describes that group; it is not a rate
  for all Canadians with endometriosis, and it is not a care protocol.

## Notable clinical signals

- **No new guideline, Cochrane update, or regulatory decision** this week.
  Nothing here changes an OpenEndo repurposing verdict. Nothing here is a
  patient-facing treatment recommendation.
- **Robot-assisted versus laparoscopy (PMID 42830120)** is the strongest
  comparative surgical item: laparoscopy was faster, the time estimate is
  highly heterogeneous, and most other perioperative outcomes were not
  separated. It does not justify switching technique.
- **Adolescent imaging (PMID 42833036)** and the **state-of-the-art imaging
  review (PMID 42838882)** describe current ultrasound-first practice. The
  scoping review's limit — a negative scan does not exclude superficial
  disease — is the claim worth keeping. Neither paper is a new guideline.
- **Two model papers mark themselves as not ready:** the deep-infiltrating
  classifier (PMID 42835735; internal AUC 0.787, single centre) and the
  endometriosis-associated ovarian-malignancy models (PMID 42835309; all
  included studies high risk of bias, two external evaluations not pooled,
  authors: routine use is premature). Those are limits on what the models can
  be used to claim. They do not change endometriosis care.
- **Cyst rupture by endometriosis subtype (PMID 42826790)** is early-stage
  ovarian-cancer surgery, retrospective and single-centre. It is not
  endometriosis treatment evidence.
- **PRDX4 (PMID 42848172)**, **SRC-2 (PMID 42836692)**, **naringenin/CXCR4
  (PMID 42842599)** and the two narrative frameworks (PMIDs 42844237,
  42840992) are preclinical or conceptual. The treatment-strategy sentences
  in those abstracts are hypotheses.
