## Diagnostics and imaging

- **Global guideline fragmentation** — scoping review (PubMed, EMBASE and
  Scopus from inception through October 2025; grey literature through March
  2026). 47 guidelines met inclusion (28 English-language, 19
  non-English). Most come from high-income settings; Europe accounts for
  15/47 (32%), with the abstract giving Africa 2% and the Eastern
  Mediterranean 4%. The abstract reports underrepresentation of adolescents
  (77%), people with lived experience (30%) and gender-diverse people (19%),
  and limited consideration of primary care physicians (49%). It does not
  define whether each percentage is a share of guidelines that include or
  omit the group, so those figures are left as the abstract states them.
  The authors describe inconsistent evidence-grading and gaps in alignment
  on histological confirmation, biomarker use, aromatase inhibitors, repeat
  surgery, fertility care, non-medical interventions and self-care. Scoping
  review of existing guidance.
  [PMID 42875470](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42875470/) — *why it
  matters:* a map of how endometriosis guidance is produced and where it
  diverges. The authors' call for a global normative guideline is their
  conclusion. This paper is not itself a guideline, and it does not replace
  any OpenEndo care statement.
- **SEED screening tool, protocol only** — planned cross-sectional
  validation in Canada. 300 people assigned female at birth, aged 18–25,
  with endometriosis or chronic pelvic pain, will complete the Screening for
  Early Endometriosis Detection tool plus convergent and divergent measures.
  Criterion validity, sensitivity and specificity are planned against
  endometriosis versus chronic pelvic pain without endometriosis, with
  sensitivity prioritised for a screening cut-off. Ethics approval is
  recorded (Women's College Hospital REB #2024-0065-E). No scores, no
  cut-off and no accuracy estimate are in this record. Protocol.
  [PMID 42869590](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42869590/) — *why it
  matters:* a validation that has not happened yet. It is not evidence that
  SEED detects endometriosis.
- **Cystic–solid ADC difference, seromucinous borderline tumour versus
  endometriosis-associated ovarian cancer** — retrospective single-centre
  MRI study, 49 pathologically confirmed cases (seromucinous borderline
  tumour n=14, endometriosis-associated ovarian cancer n=35). DeltaADC
  (cystic ADC minus solid ADC) gave AUC 0.924 (95% CI 0.817–1.000); solid
  ADC gave AUC 0.918 (95% CI 0.837–1.000); the difference was not
  significant (p=0.862). At an internally derived DeltaADC cutoff of
  0.529×10⁻³ mm²/s, sensitivity was 94.3% (95% CI 80.8–99.3) and specificity
  92.9% (95% CI 66.1–99.8). The authors state that a cutoff derived and
  tested in the same cohort can look better than it will elsewhere.
  [PMID 42862744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42862744/) — *why it
  matters:* a preoperative imaging contrast inside ovarian masses already
  tied to endometriosis, with no incremental value over solid ADC shown
  here. The performance numbers are internal. Exploratory; low certainty
  for any diagnostic claim. Independent multicentre validation is what the
  authors say is still required.
- **Why circulating miRNA markers have not reached consensus** — narrative
  review of non-invasive miRNA diagnostics, PubMed 1 January 2016 to
  1 September 2026. 238 articles were retrieved. After setting aside reviews
  and database searches (101), tissue or cell studies (46), papers not
  focused on endometriosis (24), exosome papers (17), other body fluids (15),
  AI or machine-learning papers (4) and animal studies (2), 29 publications
  remained. The abstract's point is methodological: serum/plasma isolation,
  RNA extraction, amplification, normalisation and haemolysis handling differ
  across studies, and the differentially expressed miRNAs do not agree.
  Narrative review. [PMID 42854833](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42854833/)
  — *why it matters:* a current account of why a blood miRNA test is not a
  settled diagnostic. It names no validated signature and issues no
  sensitivity or specificity of its own. Publication date 2026-10-09, so it
  sits on the boundary of the previous window; it is absent from
  `weekly/2026-10-09.json`.
- **Erratum, salivary miRNA interim validation** — PubMed classifies this
  record as a published erratum for the 2023 NEJM Evidence interim analysis
  of a 109-miRNA saliva signature (PMID 38320163; DOI
  10.1056/EVIDoa2200282). The fetched PubMed record and the Crossref
  metadata contain no correction text, so what changed is not stated here.
  [PMID 42853081](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42853081/) — *why it
  matters:* an erratum now exists on a paper that is widely cited for
  saliva-based detection. Until the correction text is read, this record
  changes no accuracy claim and no OpenEndo statement about the saliva test.
  Publication date 2026-10-09; absent from the previous snapshot.
- **Letter on a salivary microRNA clinical evaluation** — the title states
  methodological, statistical and reporting limitations of a clinical
  evaluation of salivary microRNA testing in an endometriosis centre. The
  fetched PubMed record has no abstract, and no open full text was
  available, so the specific limitations are not synthesized.
  [PMID 42880026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42880026/) — *why it
  matters:* title-level only. It is a critique flag on saliva testing, not
  a new accuracy result.

## Surgery

- **Reoperation after surgery for ovarian endometriosis** — Finnish national
  registers. Women with a first surgically verified diagnosis of ovarian
  endometriosis and an ovarian procedure at the index operation, 1987–2012
  (n=13,912), followed to 2019. Median follow-up 20.7 years (IQR 14.8–27.2).
  Kaplan–Meier 30-year cumulative risk was 17.3% for any reoperation and
  16.5% for an ovarian reoperation. Median time to the first reoperation was
  3.4 years (IQR 1.4–7.2); later reoperations came sooner (median under
  2 years). Women who were reoperated had fewer births (61.6% vs 70.7%) and
  more infertility (51.5% vs 17.1%) than women with a single surgery. The
  crude reoperation proportion was higher in the ICD-10 era than the ICD-9
  era (19.0% vs 12.4%) and occurred sooner (median 3.1 vs 4.5 years). In the
  adjusted Cox model, an ICD-10 index operation was associated with a lower
  ovarian-reoperation hazard: those are different contrasts, and this digest
  does not reconcile them. Even when the index diagnosis was ovarian
  endometriosis alone, peritoneal procedures (3.6%) and deep-infiltrating
  procedures (1.8%) were recorded; by the fourth or later operation, deep
  procedures were 13.4%. Younger age, a family history of endometriosis and
  a primary endometriosis diagnosis were associated with higher risk of any
  and of ovarian reoperation. Hysterectomy before or at the index operation
  was associated with lower risk. Infertility before the index operation was
  associated with any reoperation. Versus oophorectomy, enucleation or
  resection had aHR 1.43 (95% CI 1.28–1.60) for any reoperation, and other
  ovarian procedures had aHR 2.20 (95% CI 1.67–2.89). For ovarian
  reoperation specifically, the increase was seen for other ovarian
  procedures (aHR 1.68, 95% CI 1.23–2.31). The abstract states that
  reoperation followed ovarian-conserving procedures in about one in three
  women and oophorectomy in about one in ten. Register cohort, historical
  span, procedure choice is confounded by age, fertility intent and disease.
  [PMID 42869118](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42869118/) — *why it
  matters:* the longest reoperation description in this window, and the
  clearest statement of surgical burden. The authors' own close is
  individualised, fertility-conscious decisions that weigh ovarian
  preservation against recurrence. That framing is theirs. It is not a
  directive to remove an ovary, and it does not change OpenEndo care text.
- **Fertility after colorectal endometriosis surgery, robot versus
  laparoscopy** — retrospective single-centre cohort, operations February
  2019 to January 2023, colorectal resection, limited to patients who wanted
  pregnancy before surgery or immediately after. Among 72 patients wishing
  to conceive, pregnancy occurred in 23/34 (68%) after robotic-assisted
  laparoscopy and 25/38 (66%) after conventional laparoscopy (not
  significant). Spontaneous pregnancies were 11/23 (48%) and 9/25 (36%)
  (not significant). Among those who conceived, at least one live birth
  occurred in 18/23 (78.3%) and 21/25 (84%) (not significant). Retrospective,
  one centre, wish-to-conceive cohort.
  [PMID 42874709](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42874709/) — *why it
  matters:* the title asks whether every patient should have a robotic
  approach. In this series the fertility outcomes were not separated. It
  does not establish a robotic advantage, and it is not a reason to switch
  technique.
- **NIR-II polymeric probe for lesion navigation** — a semiconducting
  polymer (P-BPTI), formulated as nanoparticles, imaged in the second
  near-infrared window in a multi-lesion mouse model. The abstract reports
  stronger NIR-II signal, photostability and deeper penetration than the
  monomeric counterpart and indocyanine green, with image-guided resection
  and repeat imaging at 7-day intervals out to 49 days after surgery.
  Mouse study. [PMID 42634982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42634982/) —
  *why it matters:* a preclinical imaging agent for finding lesions at
  surgery. Issue date 2026-10-12 pulled it into this pdat window; entrez
  date is 2026-08-24, and it is absent from earlier weekly snapshots. It is
  not a human device and not a surgical outcome.
- **Thoracic endometriosis, three-step diagnostic series** — retrospective
  series of 3 women with recurrent right-sided catamenial pneumothorax and
  pelvic endometriosis, operated in Gdańsk, 2021–2024. The proposed sequence
  is chest CT, video-assisted thoracoscopic lesion mapping, and histology
  with estrogen-receptor and CD10 immunostaining. The abstract states that
  CT helped find bullous lesions. Case series, n=3.
  [PMID 42878335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42878335/) — *why it
  matters:* a worked example of how thoracic endometriosis was confirmed in
  three patients. Three cases do not establish a pathway.
- **Thoracic endometriosis presenting in pregnancy** — one primigravida at
  27 weeks with persistent right pneumothorax after chest drainage, and a
  history of recurrent pneumothorax and pelvic endometriosis. Video-assisted
  thoracoscopy, wedge resection and partial diaphragmatic resection.
  Histology showed endometrial glands and stroma in lung, bulla and
  diaphragm, with estrogen-receptor and CD10 staining. She delivered a
  3.2 kg infant at 40 weeks, with the abstract reporting no maternal or
  neonatal complications. Single case.
  [PMID 42877470](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42877470/) — *why it
  matters:* pregnancy did not rule thoracic endometriosis out in this
  patient. One operated case is not a protocol for pneumothorax in
  pregnancy.
- **Catamenial pneumothorax presenting as tension** — one 35-year-old
  woman without a prior endometriosis diagnosis, with cyclical chest
  symptoms. A right pneumothorax recurred at the next menses as tension
  pneumothorax. Video-assisted thoracoscopy found diaphragmatic
  perforations and endometriotic lesions. Single case.
  [PMID 42871473](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42871473/) — *why it
  matters:* thoracic endometriosis can be the first presentation, including
  as tension pneumothorax. Indexed this week with a year-only publication
  date, so the pdat query missed it. Single case.
- **Catamenial haemolacria** — one adolescent with cyclical eye bleeding and
  pain. Orbital MRI and haematologic and ophthalmologic work-ups did not
  find a structural or clotting cause. The diagnosis of ocular endometriosis
  is described as presumptive. A dienogest plus ethinyl estradiol
  combination was used; over two years the symptoms improved and occasional
  minimal bleeding remained. Single case, no histologic confirmation.
  [PMID 42866633](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42866633/) — *why it
  matters:* a rare extra-pelvic symptom pattern. The hormone combination is
  what was used in this case. It is not a treatment result.
- **Cesarean-scar endometriosis at two sites** — one 37-year-old woman with
  two prior cesareans, cyclical incisional pain and an abdominal-wall mass.
  Laparotomy, hysterectomy, bilateral salpingectomy and excision of the
  abdominal-wall mass. Histology confirmed endometriosis in both the
  Pfannenstiel scar and the lower uterine hysterotomy scar. Single case.
  [PMID 42871432](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42871432/) — *why it
  matters:* both scar sites were histologically endometriosis in this
  patient. Month-level issue date ("2026 Dec"); indexed this week. One case
  does not set a sampling rule.
- **Malignant transformation of abdominal-wall endometriosis** — one
  48-year-old woman, two decades after cesarean, with a growing scar mass.
  Histology was clear-cell carcinoma arising in abdominal-wall
  endometriosis. She had resection with mesh reconstruction and six cycles
  of albumin-bound paclitaxel, carboplatin, bevacizumab and sintilimab.
  Hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy were not performed. Lung
  metastasis was found 13 months after chemotherapy. Markers at diagnosis:
  CA125 98.6 U/mL, HE4 74.9 pmol/L. The largest CT lesion measured
  11.03 cm × 7.64 cm; the resected mass was 15 cm × 15 cm. Single case.
  [PMID 42860970](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42860970/) — *why it
  matters:* malignant transformation of scar endometriosis occurs and can
  present as clear-cell carcinoma. The drugs named are what this patient
  received. They are not a regimen. Electronic publication date in the
  record is 2026-05-15; indexed this week and absent from earlier snapshots.
- **Splenosis mimicking endometriosis after splenectomy** — one 16-year-old
  with hereditary spherocytosis, laparoscopic splenectomy at age 12
  (partial converted to total, spleen morcellated), later worsening pain.
  MRI could not separate endometriomas from splenules. Laparoscopy found
  widespread blue-black nodules; histology showed splenosis through the
  abdomen and pelvis, plus endometriosis in the right uterosacral ligament
  and ovarian fossa. Bowel-adherent nodules were left for a later operation.
  Single case.
  [PMID 42860207](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42860207/) — *why it
  matters:* after splenectomy, pelvic nodules on imaging and at laparoscopy
  can be splenic implants and endometriosis together. One surgical case.

## Mechanisms and biology

- **Stress, central sensitisation and endometriosis pain** — narrative
  review. Perceived stress is described as correlating with severity and
  pain intensity, with feedback among inflammation, chronic pain and stress
  physiology (neuroendocrine change, central sensitisation, neuroinflammation,
  network reorganisation, threat learning). The abstract says further work
  is needed before chronic stress can be treated as a cause of disease
  onset. Narrative review; no new cohort.
  [PMID 42866613](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42866613/) — *why it
  matters:* a mechanism sketch that puts pain in both the lesion and the
  nervous system. "Mechanism-based therapeutic strategies" in the abstract
  is a research agenda. Epub 2026-09-22; issue date 2026-10-16.
- **TREM2 and peritoneal macrophages** — single-cell RNA-seq of peritoneal
  macrophages from one patient with endometriosis showed higher TREM2 and
  SIRPα, correlating with each other and with an estrogen-response signal.
  In a mouse fragment-injection model, small peritoneal macrophages
  increased; Trem2 knockout recipients formed fewer and smaller lesions.
  Knockout reduced M2 polarisation and SIRPα and increased phagocytosis.
  Estrogen raised TREM2/SIRPα in RAW 264.7 cells and impaired phagocytosis;
  knockdown restored it. The authors suggest that blocking TREM2 may help
  patients. Mouse plus a one-patient sequencing observation. Issue date
  2026-10-15; entrez date 2026-08-05; absent from earlier weekly snapshots.
  [PMID 42555090](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42555090/) — *why it
  matters:* a macrophage checkpoint hypothesis with a mouse lesion-size
  result. The treatment sentence is preclinical. TREM2 is not an OpenEndo
  repurposing candidate, and this paper does not create one.
- **Resveratrol in a mouse model** — intraperitoneal hypoxia (HIF-1α in
  peritoneal exudate cells) and higher TLR4 and IL-1β in their murine
  model. Resveratrol reduced HIF-1α-positive cells and reduced lesion size
  and weight. TLR4 and IL-1β did not change. The authors report less
  autoantibody deposition in lesions of treated mice and propose
  autoantibody deposition as a mechanism. Mouse study. Issue date
  2026-10-15; entrez date 2026-05-11; absent from earlier weekly snapshots.
  [PMID 42108087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42108087/) — *why it
  matters:* a preclinical hypoxia and autoantibody hypothesis. "Promising
  therapeutic option" is the authors' phrase for a mouse experiment.
  Resveratrol is not an OpenEndo M3 candidate.
- **Mechanotransduction framework** — narrative review of endometriosis and
  adenomyosis. It places tissue stiffness next to inflammation, hormones,
  oxidative stress, angiogenesis and neurogenesis, and treats lesion
  phenotype as an interaction of origin, cell properties and local
  microenvironment. Elastography measurements exist, but the abstract says
  standardised comparisons across lesion types are limited, and that much of
  the mechanotransduction evidence comes from cell studies, models or other
  fibrotic diseases. Direct causal claims in human endometriosis and
  adenomyosis are described as not established. Narrative review.
  [PMID 42855651](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42855651/) — *why it
  matters:* a mechanics frame that sits beside last week's fibrosis-and-pain
  review (PMID 42844237) without repeating its data. Conceptual. Publication
  date 2026-10-09; absent from the previous snapshot.
- **Inflammation–fibrosis circuit** — mini review. Cyclic bleeding,
  platelets, iron, hypoxia and TGF-β, sphingosine-1-phosphate,
  Wnt/β-catenin, PI3K/AKT–ERK and Notch signals are linked to
  myofibroblasts and matrix stiffening, which the authors argue can feed
  back into inflammation, vessels and nerves. They state that fibrosis is
  neither the only driver of symptoms nor uniform across lesion types, and
  that lineage, timing and outcome attribution are uncertain. Hormonal and
  surgical treatment is described as remaining central; antifibrotic and
  immunomodulatory approaches need clinical validation. Narrative review.
  [PMID 42876998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876998/) — *why it
  matters:* another fibrosis synthesis, independent of the two reviews
  above. The therapeutic sentences are hypotheses. Year-only publication
  date; indexed this week.
- **Macrophage polarisation and metabolism** — narrative review. Macrophages
  accumulate and are heterogeneous across eutopic endometrium, peritoneal
  fluid and lesions. The abstract argues against a single M1-to-M2 switch:
  the programmes overlap (inflammatory, immunoregulatory, angiogenic,
  phagocytic, metabolic, profibrotic). Recruitment is tied to estrogen
  receptor β–NF-κB–CCL2, IL-17A, TNF-α, extracellular-vesicle microRNAs,
  lactate–METTL3–Trib1–ERK/STAT3, CCL2/CCR2 and TET3 suppression of let-7.
  Targeting glycolysis, mitochondria, lactate or epitranscriptomic
  regulators is proposed. Narrative review.
  [PMID 42873068](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42873068/) — *why it
  matters:* a macrophage map, not a drug. "Therapeutic implications" are
  untested. Month-level publication date ("2026 Oct"); indexed this week.
  It does not move a repurposing verdict.
- **Macrophage immune-fibrotic persistence, endometriosis and adenomyosis** —
  mini review. Endocrine suppression, analgesia and surgery are described as
  incomplete explanations of persistent pain, recurrence and fibrosis. The
  authors argue for testing non-hormonal, phenotype-guided
  immunopharmacology alongside hormonal suppression, with fertility-compatible
  design. Narrative review.
  [PMID 42877118](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42877118/) — *why it
  matters:* a trial-design argument, not a compound or a result. Year-only
  publication date; indexed this week. Read with PMID 42873068 and PMID
  42555090 as three separate macrophage papers, not one replicated finding.
- **Peritoneal-fluid IL-1α and IL-12** — pilot case-control study, 47
  patients at laparoscopy in Lublin. Endometriosis was laparoscopically
  confirmed; histology was available in 20 of 28 endometriosis cases
  (71.4%), and 8 superficial stage I lesions were not biopsied. Median
  peritoneal-fluid volume was about 5 mL in the endometriosis group and
  about 4 mL in the reference group. Median IL-12 was more than twice as
  high in the reference group as in the endometriosis group. The abstract
  describes higher IL-1α and lower IL-12 in the endometriosis group and
  says this pilot cannot establish the relationship. No numeric
  concentrations are given. Pilot, n=47.
  [PMID 42859589](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42859589/) — *why it
  matters:* a cytokine difference in peritoneal fluid, with incomplete
  histology and no concentrations in the abstract. Low certainty.
- **Modified Shaofu Zhuyu decoction in a rat angiogenesis model** — English
  abstract of a rat study. Endometriosis was induced, then animals were
  assigned to dienogest, high- and low-dose modified Shaofu Zhuyu decoction,
  or high-dose decoction plus a CaMKKβ inhibitor. The abstract reports
  smaller ectopic lesions, less mitochondrial reactive oxygen species, lower
  CaMKKβ/AMPK/HIF-1α and mitophagy markers (BNIP3, Bcl-2, LC3B), and lower
  serum Ang-2, VEGF and ICAM-1 versus the model group, with the combination
  described as stronger than each intervention alone. No effect sizes or
  doses appear in the abstract. Rat study.
  [PMID 42869843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42869843/) — *why it
  matters:* a preclinical herbal-formula experiment on a hypoxia and
  mitophagy pathway. It is not clinical evidence, it names no human dose,
  and it is not an OpenEndo M3 candidate. Issue date 2026 Sep; indexed this
  week.

## Epidemiology and burden

- **Endometriosis and PCOS burden across Europe, 1990–2023** — Global Burden
  of Disease 2023, women aged 15–49, 44 European countries. The analysis is
  ecological co-distribution of population rates, which the abstract
  distinguishes from individual comorbidity. Endometriosis burden
  concentrated in Central and Eastern Europe; PCOS burden in Western Europe.
  Seven countries had a consistent dual-burden pattern, 19 were
  endometriosis-dominant and 18 were PCOS-dominant. Age-standardised
  incidence of both declined across most subregions from 1990 to 2023. The
  endometriosis disability rate declined in Western Europe and was
  non-monotonic in Eastern Europe; the PCOS disability rate rose in every
  region. Endometriosis incidence was weakly and negatively correlated with
  the Socio-demographic Index; PCOS incidence and disability were positively
  correlated. Decomposition attributed PCOS disability growth mainly to
  epidemiological change and endometriosis burden reduction mainly to
  demography. GBD analysis.
  [PMID 42866635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42866635/) — *why it
  matters:* a Europe-wide burden map, not a statement that the same women
  have both diseases. Year-only publication date; indexed this week. It does
  not change prevalence figures OpenEndo already cites from WHO.
- **Endometriosis and early-onset cardiovascular disease, NHANES preprint** —
  cross-sectional analysis of NHANES 1999–2006, women aged 20–54.
  Endometriosis and early-onset cardiovascular disease (coronary disease,
  angina, heart attack, heart failure or stroke) were self-reported. The
  abstract states both 5,345 and 5,435 as the sample size; that
  inconsistency is unresolved in the record. 370 women (6.9%) reported
  endometriosis and 162 (3.0%) reported early-onset cardiovascular disease.
  After inverse-probability weighting for age, body mass index, smoking,
  education, age at menarche, physical activity and race/ethnicity, the
  prevalence odds ratio was 1.91 (95% CI 1.23–2.97). The association was
  strongest at CRP >3 mg/L (POR 2.33, 95% CI 1.49–3.66) and was not
  described at lower CRP (interaction p=0.086). Across the systemic
  immune-inflammation index, the medium tertile was POR 2.92 (95% CI
  1.47–5.78), with no association in the low or high tertiles (interaction
  p=0.288). The authors call the inflammation interaction exploratory.
  Preprint (medRxiv), not peer-reviewed.
  [PMID 42856635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42856635/) — *why it
  matters:* a US self-report signal in the same direction as the Beijing
  claims cohort synthesised on 2026-09-25 (adjusted HR 1.69, PMID 42757591).
  It is a different design, the interaction tests are not significant, and
  the sample-size discrepancy is a reason to wait for the version of record.
  It does not establish that endometriosis causes early cardiovascular
  disease, and it does not change care guidance. Posted 2026-09-30; indexed
  this week.
- **Tubal endometriosis in hydrosalpinx salpingectomy specimens** —
  cross-sectional study of 70 infertile women undergoing laparoscopic
  salpingectomy at one centre. Histologic endometriosis was present in 10/70
  (14.3%). Among 50 women without a prior endometriosis history, 4 (8%) had
  tubal disease on histology. Visual suspicion at laparoscopy was associated
  with positive histology at the margin of significance (37.5% vs 7.4%,
  p=0.050). Clinical pregnancy after salpingectomy was 12/47 (25.5%). None
  of the women with histologic tubal endometriosis conceived, versus 30%
  among other pathologies. The authors call that contrast exploratory and
  unadjusted for age, ovarian reserve and assisted-reproduction factors.
  Cross-sectional, n=70.
  [PMID 42877553](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42877553/) — *why it
  matters:* hidden tubal endometriosis showed up in salpingectomy specimens,
  including in women without a prior diagnosis. The zero-pregnancy
  observation is a small, unadjusted subgroup. Low certainty.

## Pharmacology and supportive care

- **Gestrinone among the progestins** — narrative review with no
  prespecified protocol (PubMed, trial registries, regulatory sources; no
  date or language limit). Gestrinone is described as an androgenic
  19-nortestosterone progestin used for about four decades in endometriosis
  and other estrogen-dependent conditions, and as largely absent from
  current progestin taxonomies. The abstract says it was active in every
  controlled setting the authors examined. Direct comparisons with danazol
  and with a GnRH agonist found no difference in the principal pain
  outcomes, in trials too small to establish equivalence. No direct
  comparison with dienogest is reported. The androgenic-metabolic signature
  is described as the hallmark, and androgenic effects as not consistently
  milder than danazol. Bone findings varied by study and dose and, in the
  authors' words, do not establish long-term skeletal protection or that
  add-back can be skipped. Narrative review.
  [PMID 42878410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42878410/) — *why it
  matters:* a pharmacology update on an older drug, consistent with the
  limit already recorded from the Cochrane progesterone-receptor-modulator
  review (PMID 42746927, digest 2026-09-18): gestrinone comparisons are
  uncertain. This is not a new trial, not a guideline, and not a reason to
  prefer gestrinone. No OpenEndo treatment text changes.
- **Low-FODMAP diet, systematic review and meta-analysis** — PubMed, Embase
  and Cochrane; randomised and observational studies; outcome EHP-30.
  Three studies, 90 women, mean age 34 years, mean follow-up 3.5 months.
  The pooled figures are post-intervention mean scores, not differences
  versus a control diet and not changes from baseline: pain 28.45 (95% CI
  20.88–36.02; I²=61.8%), control/helplessness 39.33 (95% CI
  24.92–53.73; I²=81.7%), emotional well-being 31.87 (95% CI 27.15–36.59;
  I²=0%), social support 38.94 (95% CI 33.69–44.20; I²=0%), self-image
  43.22 (95% CI 35.94–50.49; I²=21.2%). The authors conclude that a
  low-FODMAP diet was associated with improvement and immediately label the
  finding hypothesis-generating, pending larger randomised trials.
  Systematic review and meta-analysis.
  [PMID 42876029](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876029/) — *why it
  matters:* the headline verb is "improvement", and the numbers provided are
  absolute scores after the diet. That is a weak basis for a symptom claim.
  It is not evidence that a low-FODMAP diet should be used, and OpenEndo
  does not gain a dietary recommendation from it. Month-level publication
  date ("2026 Oct"); indexed this week.
- **Acupuncture acupoint data-mining, protocol only** — planned search of

  acupuncture clinical trials, with association rules, clustering, factor
  analysis and decision trees. No acupoints are named, because the study
  has not been run. Protocol.
  [PMID 42877258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42877258/) — *why it
  matters:* a methods plan for summarising published point choices. The

## Notable clinical signals

- **No new guideline, Cochrane update, regulatory decision, or phase III
  readout** this week. No OpenEndo repurposing verdict moves. No
  knowledge-page care statement is edited in this digest.
- **Reoperation (PMID 42869118)** is the strongest clinical epidemiology:
  about one in six women in a 13,912-woman Finnish register cohort had a
  recorded reoperation by 30 years after ovarian-endometriosis surgery, and
  ovarian-conserving procedures carried a higher adjusted hazard than
  oophorectomy. The cohort is historical, procedure choice is confounded,
  and the authors frame the trade-off as individual and fertility-conscious.
  It does not justify a default operation.
- **Guideline scoping review (PMID 42875470)** documents fragmented,
  high-income-weighted guidance across 47 documents. It is a review of
  guidelines, not a guideline to implement.
- **Low-FODMAP (PMID 42876029)** pools post-intervention EHP-30 means from
  three studies (90 women). The authors' improvement language is ahead of
  the contrast the meta-analysis actually reports. Hypothesis-generating, as
  they say.
- **Robotic versus conventional laparoscopy after colorectal resection
  (PMID 42874709)** did not separate pregnancy or live birth (72 women
  wishing to conceive). The title's question — whether everyone should have
  a robotic approach — is not answered in the affirmative by these data.
- **Gestrinone (PMID 42878410)** restates an older androgenic progestin
  without a dienogest comparison and without trials large enough for
  equivalence against danazol or a GnRH agonist. It sits beside the Cochrane
  limit from digest 2026-09-18 (PMID 42746927). It is not a new option to
  adopt.
- **Saliva and blood miRNA** produced a methods review (PMID 42854833), an
  erratum whose correction text is not in the PubMed record (PMID 42853081,
  for PMID 38320163), and a letter with no abstract (PMID 42880026). None of
  the three supplies a new accuracy figure. The September note on the saliva
  Endotest (PMID 42749315, digest 2026-09-18) is unchanged.
- **SEED (PMID 42869590)** and the **acupuncture mining protocol (PMID
  42877258)** are protocols. No performance and no acupoint list.
- **Early cardiovascular disease (PMID 42856635)** is a preprint,

- **Extra-pelvic and scar cases** (PMIDs 42878335, 42877470, 42871473,
