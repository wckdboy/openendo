# Ryeqo vs Yselty — practical comparison (updated 2026-09-02)

Both are oral GnRH-antagonists approved in the EU for **second-line** symptomatic endometriosis (previous medical or surgical treatment). See [[gnrh-antagonists]].

| Dimension | Ryeqo (relugolix combo) | Yselty (linzagolix) |
|---|---|---|
| EU approval, endometriosis | Nov 2023 | Dec 2024 |
| Add-back | Built into the pill (40+1+0.5 mg) | Separate tablet; **200 mg + add-back required** for the endometriosis indication |
| Evidence | SPIRIT 1+2 (n=1,261) | EDELWEISS 3+6 (n=484) |
| Dysmenorrhea responders | ~75% vs ~27% placebo (24 wks) | 72.9% vs 23.5% at month 3; 91.0% at month 12 (200 mg + ABT) |
| No-hormone option | No | **Not for endometriosis** — 100 mg-no-ABT flexibility is fibroid-label only (75 mg trial arm not a marketed dose) |
| DK access | **Generelt tilskud**, pharmacy-stocked | **No general reimbursement for endometriosis** (fibroid-only clause); specialist prescription, NBS |
| DK cost | ~33–35 DKK/day (reimbursed) | Full price out-of-pocket (DK list price not published; UK ~£1,100/yr) |

## Verdict

**For a Danish endometriosis patient today: Ryeqo is the practical choice** — approved, reimbursed, stocked. **Yselty is the specialist conversation** for women who cannot use an add-back-containing GnRH-antagonist — but note the label requires add-back at the endometriosis dose, so the "no-hormone" argument for choosing Yselty is weaker than often assumed. If Yselty is pursued, expect full price or a regional *enkelttilskud* application, and verify pharmacy stock. England's NICE (TA1067) lists linzagolix as an option.^[https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ta1067]
