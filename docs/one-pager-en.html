OpenEndo · fact sheet for decision-makers · September 2026

Endometriosis is one of the most common diseases on Earth.
It is also one of the most ignored.

The essential numbers, the treatments that exist, and the three questions every health minister, funder and politician should be able to answer.
⏰ Deadline that matters: 22 September 2026 — US DoD PRMRP Clinical Trial Award applications close. Endometriosis has repeatedly been a funded topic. Ask your institutions: who is applying — and for what?

1.The numbers that should outrage everyone

  • ~190 million women affected worldwide — 1 in 10 of reproductive age. WHO
  • 7–10 years average time to diagnosis. In that time the disease spreads, fertility is affected, and pain becomes chronic — while symptoms are dismissed as "just bad periods". Endometriosis UK · multiple studies
  • No cure. Treatment is hormonal suppression, surgery and pain management — with frequent recurrence. ClinicalTrials.gov · medical guidelines
  • €69 billion per year — global cost of lost productivity and treatment. DDDT review 2025
  • A funding gap of shame: endometriosis affects roughly as many people as diabetes — yet receives a fraction of the research funding. The result: no non-invasive diagnostic standard, no cure, treatments unchanged in decades.

2.What actually works — and what's new

💊 GnRH antagonist combination pills

Relugolix CT (Ryeqo/Myfembree) — approved in the EU (2023) and US for endometriosis after prior treatment. SPIRIT trials (n=1,261): significantly more responders on menstrual and pelvic pain — and more women off analgesics entirely by week 24. Linzagolix (Yselty) — EU-approved 2024; a 75 mg dose works without hormonal add-back.

EMA · FDA · SPIRIT 1+2 · EDELWEISS 3

🏥 Specialist excision surgery

Laparoscopic excision by a specialist team remains the gold standard for severe disease. Outcomes vary enormously with surgeon expertise — yet surgery is rarely centralized, and re-operation rates are high.

ESHRE guideline · surgical literature

🧪 Non-invasive diagnostics are here — but not reimbursed

Saliva-based miRNA tests (CE-IVD) can cut the 7–10 year delay dramatically. They exist, they work, and most health systems still don't cover them — laparoscopy remains the "standard".

Ziwig Endotest · independent validation studies

🔬 The pipeline finally moves

HMI-115 — the first non-hormonal therapy ever in Phase III (anti-prolactin-receptor antibody; pain relief without menopausal side effects; FDA Fast Track). ENDO-205 — a "precision peptide" designed to eliminate lesions, IND-cleared 2026.

ClinicalTrials.gov · Lancet OGWH

3.Why the status quo is a policy failure

  • Women's pain is documented as taken less seriously than men's — for endometriosis this compounds into years of birth-control prescriptions without imaging, and emergency visits dismissed as anxiety. Chen et al. 2008 · patient surveys
  • The disease is invisible in medical education, research budgets and clinical guidelines — relative to its burden, it is among the most underfunded chronic diseases in the world.
  • What works is known. What is missing is political will, centralized care and funded research — not more awareness campaigns.

4.Three asks for decision-makers

  1. Fund cures, not just symptom control. Prioritize non-hormonal therapies, lesion-targeting research and diagnostic tools — in national research centres and military/health research programs (e.g. US DoD PRMRP, deadline 22 Sep 2026; EU Horizon Europe calls).
  2. Put non-invasive diagnostics into clinical pathways — and reimburse them. A 7–10 year diagnostic delay is a human rights and economic scandal; saliva/imaging tests already exist.
  3. Set national diagnostic-delay targets (e.g. ≤1 year), centralize specialist surgery, and count endometriosis in health statistics and medical curricula properly.
